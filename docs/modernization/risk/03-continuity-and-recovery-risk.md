# Continuity and Recovery Risk

This document records what LIFE400 does and does not guarantee about data integrity, durability and recoverability. It is the third of the three documents answering the first of this assessment's two business drivers — minimize security risk — and it carries the integrity and availability half of that driver, where [the security risk register](01-security-risk-register.md) carries the confidentiality and access half. It exists because rollback and reconciliation for this estate have to be **designed rather than assumed**: the application holds no transaction boundary, no change history and no restore path of its own, so every recovery property a migration will depend on is a property this estate does not currently have. Seven risks are recorded below as `CR-01` through `CR-07`, each anchored in a source member a reader can open or in a search a reader can repeat.

**Scope.** This document establishes premises and stops. It prescribes **no procedure**: the cutover steps, the rollback triggers and the reconciliation sequence belong to [the cutover and rollback document](../migration/07-cutover-and-rollback.md) — planned and not yet written, and the named consumer of everything below — and the design of the live comparison belongs to [the parallel run and output parity document](../migration/06-parallel-run-and-output-parity.md), also planned. The choice to migrate incrementally alongside the running system rather than in one cut — a choice these findings are a primary driver of, because there is no safety net to cut away from — is to be recorded in the planned [MOD-ADR-002](../decisions/MOD-ADR-002-migration-pattern.md). It **defines no recovery objective**, because there is none in the repository to record and inventing one would be a fabricated figure dressed as a measurement; that no objective exists is itself finding `CR-05`. It does not re-derive the mechanics of the orchestration layer — the work-management objects, the [CPF message](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#cpf-message) vocabulary, override scope, queue serialization, the nightly job's telemetry and the eight-step build sequence are all owned by [the operational model](../current-state/06-operational-model.md) and are referenced rather than repeated. It assigns no migrate, implement or drop verb to anything; every disposition in this assessment is the sole business of [the known defects and stubs register](../current-state/07-known-defects-and-stubs.md). It asserts no regulatory framework and performs no retention analysis, both of which belong to [the compliance and data protection document](02-compliance-and-data-protection.md). And it applies no remediation of any kind: no source member, schema definition or build artifact is modified anywhere in this assessment, and naming a capability the target needs is not the same as building one.

**Reading the citations.** A citation of the form `[<path>:<locator>]` is plain text rather than a hyperlink, and points at a path in this repository. Plain text is chosen so that a citation stays meaningful in a diff and does not depend on a hosting provider's line-anchor syntax. Every line and range cited below was opened and read against the working tree while this document was written; none is carried forward from a prior description. Where a claim rests on an **absence**, the search that establishes it is stated in full — the strings searched and the set of members searched — so that a reader can repeat it and reach the same answer. Platform terminology links to [the IBM i glossary](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md) on first use in this document. Two terms this document needs carry no glossary entry, and both are therefore defined inline where they first appear: *commitment control* and *journaling*. That they are absent from a glossary built from the estate's own vocabulary is consistent with what `CR-02` and `CR-03` measure — no member of the estate contains either construct — which is why they are defined here rather than added there.

**What could not be verified, stated once and up front.** Nothing below was validated by building or running anything, and nothing could be. ILE COBOL and ILE CL require the platform, every program in the estate names that platform as its object computer as well as its source computer [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L28-L29], [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L27-L28], and the repository holds no test suite to run in place of an execution. That matters most for the control-flow reading under `CR-01`, which states which statements a paragraph reaches on which path: that reading was established **by reading the source, statement by statement**, and is presented as a reading of the code rather than as an observed behaviour. No claim in this document should be taken as having been confirmed by a compile, a bind, a run or a test.

## How to read a risk

Each risk below is a section, and every section is summarised in [the table at the end](#the-continuity-risks-at-a-glance) so that the executive recommendation can lift a row without restating its evidence. A risk has four parts:

- **Finding** — what the estate does or does not guarantee, stated as a property of the estate rather than as a judgement about it.
- **Evidence** — inline citations, or a stated and repeatable search where the finding is an absence.
- **Consequence for recovery** — what follows for rollback, reconciliation and continuity. Reasoned from the evidence and never extended beyond it.
- **What the migration must therefore provide** — the capability the target needs, expressed as a requirement rather than as a design or a procedure. Where a capability is designed elsewhere, the owning document is linked.

**One distinction runs through every finding and is the reason this document can be trusted as a premise.** An absence *in the source* is provable here: the members are in this repository, the search is stated, and the result is reproducible. An absence *in an installation* is not provable here at all. Backups and journaling are configured against installed objects on a running system, outside these members, so this repository can establish only that the estate never asks for them. Each finding below says which of the two it is, and every claim of the second kind is marked as requiring business confirmation rather than resolved by assumption in either direction. Nothing is inferred from silence.

## CR-01 — Non-atomic two-file writes

**Finding.** Two interactive paths each update two files with two independent statements and nothing around the pair. There is no transaction boundary, no status test between the statements and no compensating action, so an interruption between the first statement and the second leaves the policy master changed and the dependent record missing. **There are two such pairs, not one**, and they are in different business domains.

**Evidence — the two pairs.** A census of the file-changing verbs across the code lines of all eight ILE COBOL members finds exactly two places where a single path changes two different *database* files. Both are the interactive program of their domain, and the claims pair carries a comment naming both destinations.

| Domain | Program and paragraph | First statement | Second statement | Statements between them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Policy servicing | `SVCMNT`, `4000-APPLY-AMENDMENT` [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L149] | `REWRITE WS-POLICY-MASTER-REC` [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L190] | `WRITE SVCPF-RECORD` [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L191] | none |
| Death claims | `CLMMNT`, `4000-ADJUDICATE-CLAIM` [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L149] | `REWRITE WS-POLICY-MASTER-REC` [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L178] | `WRITE CLMPF-RECORD` [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L179] | none, under the comment `* WRITE POLMST AND CLMPF` [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L177] |

In both cases the two statements are adjacent, the paragraph then displays its result and ends [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L192], [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L180], and no construct of any kind encloses the pair. The exposure is symmetrical in structure and identical in kind: a policy amendment recorded against a policy whose servicing record was never written, or a settled claim recorded against a policy whose claim record was never written.

**Evidence — neither pair can detect its own partial failure.** This is the property that makes the finding worse than the absence of a transaction, and it is measured rather than inferred. Both programs declare a file-status item for every file they open, so the mechanism to notice a failed write exists in the source and is simply never used on these paths.

| Program | Status item for the secondary file | References after its declaration | Where the policy-master status *is* tested | Failure phrase on either write |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `SVCMNT` | `WS-SVCPF-STATUS` [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L63], bound at [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L49] | none — the declaration is its only occurrence in the member | once, in the main loop after the header read [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L85] | none; the member's only `INVALID KEY` phrases sit on the keyed read [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L114], [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L116] |
| `CLMMNT` | `WS-CLMPF-STATUS` [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L62], bound at [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L48] | none — the declaration is its only occurrence in the member | once, in the main loop after the header read [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L81] | none; the member's only `INVALID KEY` phrases sit on the keyed read [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L114], [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L116] |

Read together those rows say something precise. Neither `REWRITE` nor `WRITE` on either path carries a failure phrase, and neither is followed by a test of the status item bound to the file it wrote. The programs therefore cannot distinguish a pair in which both writes succeeded from a pair in which the first succeeded and the second did not, and in both cases the paragraph proceeds to display a result to the operator [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L192], [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L180]. **A partial write is not merely unrecoverable; it is undetected at the point it happens, and reported to the operator as an outcome.** For a rollback designer that is the load-bearing consequence: there is no signal in the system from which the set of affected keys could later be assembled.

**Evidence — the two pairs fail differently, and one generic rollback rule cannot cover both.** This is the most useful single thing this document can hand to the cutover runbook, and it is a reading of the two paragraphs rather than an observation of two runs.

- **The servicing pair is not conditioned on the result code.** `4000-APPLY-AMENDMENT` guards exactly one thing at its head: a claimed or terminated contract sets result code 11, displays, and leaves the paragraph [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L155-L161]. Past that single guard, every path reaches the writes. The amendment-type dispatch sets a result code and falls through — including its `WHEN OTHER` branch, which sets code 12 for an unrecognised amendment type and does not exit [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L186-L188] — and the sub-paragraphs it performs exit only themselves and return to `4000`, as at the plan-change guard that sets code 13 [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L199] and the age guard that sets code 14 and restores the prior plan code [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L210]. No sub-paragraph can leave the program instead: the member's only `GOBACK` is in its main loop [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L103], and it is the sole occurrence of that verb in the member. Because the writes at [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L190-L191] test nothing, **a rejected amendment persists both records**, and the servicing file therefore accumulates a record for requests the program itself declined.
- **The claims pair is guarded on every path.** `4000-ADJUDICATE-CLAIM` takes four separate exits before it can reach its writes — a hard validation failure, a documents-incomplete referral to manual review, an investigation referral and a coverage rejection, each displaying its result and leaving the paragraph [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L154-L173] — and only a claim that has been calculated and settled [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L175-L176] arrives at [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L178-L179]. Every early exit persists nothing at all.

The two paths therefore leave *different* residues behind, and a reversal rule written for one is wrong for the other: on the servicing path an existing servicing record does not imply an accepted amendment, while on the claims path an existing claim record does imply a settled claim. This document records the control flow the source shows and the state each path leaves; it does not speculate about why the two were written differently, and it assigns no verb — the divergence family this belongs to is dispositioned in [the known defects and stubs register](../current-state/07-known-defects-and-stubs.md), and the complete online-versus-batch divergence inventory is owned by [the current-state architecture](../current-state/02-architecture-current-state.md).

**Evidence — the batch paths carry no two-file exposure.** The exposure is specific to the two interactive paths. Every other mutating program in the estate changes the policy master only: the batch claims engine on four separate paths [QCBLLESRC/CLMADJB.cbl:L100], [QCBLLESRC/CLMADJB.cbl:L107], [QCBLLESRC/CLMADJB.cbl:L114], [QCBLLESRC/CLMADJB.cbl:L120], the batch new-business engine on three [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L97], [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L106], [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L115], the batch servicing engine on two [QCBLLESRC/SVCBILB.cbl:L109], [QCBLLESRC/SVCBILB.cbl:L123], and the interactive new-business program on one, whichever of the two branches of its keyed read is taken [QCBLLESRC/NBUWMNT.cbl:L198-L203]. The read-only inquiry program opens the policy master for input alone [QCBLLESRC/POLMSTINQ.cbl:L63] and the menu program opens no database file at all. The two batch programs that *declare* a secondary file never write it: the same census finds no write to the servicing file anywhere in the batch servicing engine and no write to the claims file anywhere in the batch claims engine, and the servicing engine's validation-failure path illustrates the shape — it rewrites the master and returns before its dispatch is ever reached [QCBLLESRC/SVCBILB.cbl:L106-L112]. The full write-site census is owned by [the current-state architecture](../current-state/02-architecture-current-state.md) and is referenced here rather than reproduced.

**Why one pair of lines is cited by three documents.** The two statements at [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L190-L191] appear in [the security risk register](01-security-risk-register.md) as `SEC-07`, in [the known defects and stubs register](../current-state/07-known-defects-and-stubs.md) as an entry with a disposition, and here. They are the same two statements read for three different properties: `SEC-07` reads them for attribution — this is the estate's dedicated amendment path and it stamps no user identity on either destination — the defects register decides what happens to them, and this document reads them for atomicity. The audit framing is `SEC-07`'s and is not re-argued here; it is named so that a reader who meets the same anchor three times knows the three readings are deliberate rather than duplicated.

**Consequence for recovery.** There is no unit of work to reverse, no record of which pairs were interrupted, and no way for the application to have noticed. Any reversal is therefore a data-repair exercise performed from outside the application, over records the application cannot identify for it, on two paths whose residues differ.

**What the migration must therefore provide.** A real transaction boundary around every multi-file mutation, so that a policy change and its dependent record either both persist or neither does; a failure path that surfaces rather than swallows a rejected write; and one persistence contract per domain, so that whether a record exists means the same thing however the request arrived. The boundary itself is a target-architecture concern, and the decision that each domain has a single implementation rather than a hand-synchronised online and batch pair is to be recorded in the planned [MOD-ADR-004](../decisions/MOD-ADR-004-single-domain-rules-service.md).

## CR-02 — No commitment control

**Finding.** *Commitment control* is the platform facility by which several record changes are grouped into one unit of work and then committed or backed out together. **LIFE400 does not use it at all.** This is not weak or partial coverage; the facility is absent from the estate in every place it could appear.

**Evidence.** The measurement is a search, and it is stated so it can be repeated. Searching the eight ILE COBOL members — `QCBLLESRC/MAINMENU.cbl`, `QCBLLESRC/NBUWMNT.cbl`, `QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl`, `QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl`, `QCBLLESRC/SVCBILB.cbl`, `QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl`, `QCBLLESRC/CLMADJB.cbl` and `QCBLLESRC/POLMSTINQ.cbl` — for `COMMIT` and for `ROLLBACK` returns **zero occurrences of each**, in comments as well as in code. The same absence is recorded independently by [the current-state architecture](../current-state/02-architecture-current-state.md), which reaches it by the same census; the two measurements agree.

Two corroborating searches complete the row, because a unit of work can be established from outside a program as well as inside one:

- Searching the five ILE CL members — `QCLSRC/STRTLIFE.clle`, `QCLSRC/RUNNBUW.clle`, `QCLSRC/RUNSVC.clle`, `QCLSRC/RUNCLM.clle` and `QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle` — for `STRCMTCTL` and `ENDCMTCTL` returns **zero occurrences of each**. No job in the estate starts a commitment definition before it calls a program, and none ends one afterwards.
- Searching the documented build sequence for the same two commands returns zero. The eight steps create the library [README.md:L125], the [source physical files](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#source-physical-file-and-source-member) [README.md:L127-L130], the database files [README.md:L140-L143], the display and printer files [README.md:L149-L154], the sixteen commands that produce the eight COBOL programs [README.md:L160-L176], the ten that produce the five CL programs [README.md:L182-L192] and the four supporting objects [README.md:L199-L206] — and not one command in the sequence [README.md:L120-L218] establishes a commitment environment or opens any file under commitment control.

**A note on the strength of this claim.** Prior descriptions of this estate say that multi-file updates are not atomic. The measurement above is stronger and does not contradict it: the facility is not merely unused on the two-file paths, it is absent from all twenty-four members and from the build. Stating it as a measured absolute rather than as a weakness is what makes it usable as a premise, because a rollback designer needs to know there is nothing to configure, enable or tune — there is only something to build.

**Consequence for recovery.** With no commitment definition there is **no unit of work**, so there is no boundary to roll back to and no partial-failure recovery primitive available to the application at all. Every record change is durable the instant that individual write completes, independently of every other change in the same logical operation. The `CR-01` pairs are the sharpest illustration rather than the whole of it: the same property means a single-file batch rewrite cannot be reversed either, and a job that fails partway through a sequence of policies leaves every policy it already rewrote committed.

**What the migration must therefore provide.** A transactional datastore and an explicit unit of work per business operation, so that atomicity is a property of the operation rather than of the order in which statements happen to complete. The datastore choice is to be recorded in the planned [MOD-ADR-003](../decisions/MOD-ADR-003-target-datastore.md) and its structure in [the target data model and schema mapping](../target-state/03-target-data-model-and-schema-mapping.md).

## CR-03 — No journaling

**Finding.** *Journaling* is the platform facility that records every change made to a file in a separate journal, so that changes can afterwards be reapplied forward or removed backward, and so that a change history exists independently of the file itself. **Nothing in this estate asks for journaling.** No journal object is created, no file is placed under a journal, and no member mentions the facility.

**Evidence.** Searching all five ILE CL members and the whole of `README.md` for `STRJRNPF`, `CRTJRN` and `CRTJRNRCV` returns **zero occurrences of each**. The eight-step build sequence [README.md:L120-L218] creates every object the system needs and establishes no journal at any point in it — not alongside the three [physical files](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#physical-file) and the [logical file](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#logical-file) [README.md:L140-L143], which is where a journal would be started, and not anywhere else. Extending the same search to every DDS member returns zero as well: no member requests journaling as a file attribute.

**Where this claim stops, and why the boundary is the point.** Journaling is an attribute of an *installed file object*, applied by an administrator on a running system, not something a DDS member or a COBOL program contains. So this repository can establish only that the estate never asks for it. It cannot establish that a given file on a given machine has not been journaled by someone outside these members, and this document asserts no such thing. The same boundary is drawn in the same terms by [the current-state architecture](../current-state/02-architecture-current-state.md), deliberately, so that neither document can be read as claiming more than the source supports. **Whether journaling is in fact configured on any installation of LIFE400 is a confirm-with-the-business item**, and the finding as stated is that the system as delivered does not arrange it.

**Consequence for recovery.** As delivered, the estate has no forward-recovery and no backward-recovery source, and no change history at all. Three consequences follow directly and each is a premise the cutover runbook needs. A prior state cannot be reconstructed, because nothing records what the previous value was — the policy master's own audit column [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L78] holds at most a single most-recent stamp and is overwritten by every subsequent change, and what actually reaches it is `SEC-06`'s subject rather than this document's. An incident cannot be reconstructed after the fact, because there is no sequence of changes to replay or inspect. And a partial `CR-01` pair cannot be identified retrospectively even in principle, because no record exists of the write that did succeed.

**What the migration must therefore provide.** A durable change history that is independent of the current row — capturing what changed, from what value, by which authenticated principal and when — and one that is a first-class requirement rather than an operational option, because it is simultaneously the recovery source, the reconciliation source and the audit trail whose absence is registered as `SEC-06` and `SEC-07`. How it is presented as an audit control is designed in [the target security control design](../target-state/04-security-control-design.md).

## CR-04 — No scripted backup and no restore procedure

**Finding.** The estate contains no backup job, no restore procedure and no documented recovery step. Nothing in the repository saves the data files, and nothing describes how they would be brought back.

**Evidence.** Searching all five ILE CL members and the whole of `README.md` for `SAVLIB`, `SAVOBJ`, `RSTLIB` and `RSTOBJ` returns **zero occurrences of each**. There is consequently no save in the nightly job, which is the one scheduled unit of work the estate has: that job retrieves the system date, announces its start, sets the [library list](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#library-list), issues two file redirections, makes one call, reports, deletes the redirections and ends [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L45-L105] — and no step of it saves anything. The build sequence creates objects and starts the system [README.md:L120-L218] and describes no save, no restore and no recovery step at any point; its final step calls the interactive entry program and stops [README.md:L216-L218].

**Where this claim stops.** As with `CR-03`, the distinction between the source and the installation is decisive and is stated rather than glossed. Platform-level saves — a system save policy, a backup product, a save-while-active arrangement — are configured outside these members and could exist on any given installation. This repository establishes that **the estate as delivered arranges none**, and no more than that. **Whether a backup regime exists, what it covers and whether a restore from it has ever been exercised are confirm-with-the-business items.**

**Consequence for recovery.** This absence compounds with `CR-03` rather than merely sitting beside it. With neither a save that the estate arranges nor a journal that the estate requests, the recovery source for the policy master is **undefined by the system as delivered**: there is no stated artifact to restore from and no stated point to restore to. Two further consequences follow. A restore, if one exists, has an unknown granularity — nothing in the repository establishes whether the three data files and the logical file over the policy master would be recovered to a mutually consistent point, which matters because the relationships between them are conventions in program code rather than declarations in the schema. And a rollback of a migration stage cannot lean on a restore that is not established to exist, which is why rollback for this estate has to be designed as a data operation in its own right rather than delegated to a platform save.

**What the migration must therefore provide.** A defined and, crucially, **regularly exercised** recovery source, with a stated scope and a stated consistency point, and a restore that is tested rather than presumed. This document names the requirement and stops: it writes no backup script and prescribes no schedule, and the procedure that uses the capability belongs to [the cutover and rollback document](../migration/07-cutover-and-rollback.md), which is planned.

## CR-05 — Undefined recovery objectives

**Finding.** No recovery time objective, recovery point objective, retention period, availability target or maintenance window is stated anywhere in this repository. The estate expresses no expectation about how quickly it should be restored, how much work it may lose, how long data is kept or how available it is meant to be.

**Evidence.** The finding rests on an absence over a fully enumerated set, so the set is stated. `README.md` is the only hand-authored document in the estate outside the modernization set, and it describes the domains and programs, the product table [README.md:L62-L66], the build sequence [README.md:L120-L218], the 5250 screen reference [README.md:L226-L239], the business-rule summaries and the Y2K note [README.md:L258-L259] — and no objective, target, window or period of any kind. Extending the search to every one of the twenty-four source members returns nothing either: the only durations expressed anywhere in the estate are business rules, such as a grace period or a reinstatement window, which are properties of an insurance contract rather than of a service level, and which the [business rule inventory](../current-state/05-business-rule-inventory.md) owns.

**This document proposes no values, and that is a deliberate discipline rather than an omission.** A recovery objective is a business decision about acceptable loss and acceptable downtime; it is not derivable from source code, and any figure written here would be an invention presented as an analysis. **The finding is that the objectives are undefined, and defining them is a confirm-with-the-business item that this document exists to surface.** Everything downstream depends on it: a rollback trigger, a parity tolerance and a cutover go-or-no-go all need a stated objective to be measured against, and none of them can be settled while the target is blank.

### Growth is bounded by nothing the estate declares

Capacity belongs in this document as well as in the data-protection analysis, because an unbounded file is an availability risk and not only a retention one. No file-creation command in the documented build specifies a size — neither the three physical files nor the logical file over the policy master [README.md:L140-L143] — and no DDS member declares a capacity attribute either; the only size keyword anywhere in `QDDSSRC/` is the page geometry of the two printer files, which concerns paper rather than storage. The declaration-level detail behind both statements is owned by [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md). That combines with a second measured absence: searching the code lines of all eight COBOL members for `DELETE` returns **zero occurrences**, so no program in LIFE400 can remove a record from any file. Records therefore accumulate for as long as the files exist, with no purge, no archive and no erasure path anywhere in the estate.

The continuity consequence, which is the only part of this that belongs to this document, is that the growth curve of the three data files is unmanaged and unmonitored, and the estate provides no lever to bend it. Nothing declares an expected size, nothing measures the actual one, and no maintenance operation reduces it. Retention exposure, data minimisation and erasure are analysed by [the compliance and data protection document](02-compliance-and-data-protection.md), which owns that framing and the framework-applicability question along with it.

### The nightly run cannot be restarted, and cannot be shown to have failed

The one scheduled unit of work in the estate has no checkpoint, no restart point and no reliable failure signal. Each element of that is a line rather than an inference.

- **No checkpoint and no restart point.** The nightly job's processing is a single call carrying two sentinel literals [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L75]. There is no loop, no cursor and no position marker in the member, so there is no notion of *how far* a run got and therefore nothing to resume from. That the sweep is a stub, and that the estate has no sequential access pattern from which an iteration could be assembled, is established and dispositioned elsewhere — by [the operational model](../current-state/06-operational-model.md) and [the known defects and stubs register](../current-state/07-known-defects-and-stubs.md) respectively. The recoverability consequence is this document's: a re-run cannot be scoped, because a partial run cannot be described.
- **A failure is caught and downgraded rather than raised.** A single catch-all monitor covers the call [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L76]; on any failure it increments an error counter [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L77] and sends a diagnostic message to the application message queue [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L78-L80]. Control then continues past the monitor block, and the job proceeds to its completion reporting [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L87-L95] and ends normally [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L105]. **A failed nightly run therefore announces `DLYUPD COMPLETE` to the system operator and to the application message queue, and ends without an abnormal completion.**
- **The one number that would say how much work was done never reaches a message.** An update counter is declared at zero [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L32] and is never incremented anywhere in the member; it is copied once into a character variable prepared to carry it into a message [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L87], and that variable is then never referenced again, because the completion text is assembled from the process date and the error count alone [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L88-L89]. The reference-count analysis behind that is owned by [the operational model](../current-state/06-operational-model.md).

Put together, the recoverability position is precise: an operator who needs to know whether to re-run has one non-zero digit inside an informational completion message to go on, no statement of how many policies were processed, no indication of which ones, and no safe point to re-run from. And because `CR-02` means every rewrite the run did complete is already durable, a naive re-run is not obviously safe either.

**What the migration must therefore provide.** Stated and agreed recovery objectives against which a rollback trigger and a cutover decision can be measured; a declared retention and capacity position for every data set; and scheduled work that is restartable — idempotent, checkpointed, and able to fail loudly enough that the failure is not indistinguishable from success. The target's scheduling and observability design belongs to [the target architecture](../target-state/01-target-architecture.md).

## CR-06 — A single production library, and no environment to rehearse recovery in

**Finding.** The estate is one production library with no development, test or staging counterpart, and the library name is a compile-time literal in the source that references it. There is nowhere to rehearse a restore, nowhere to validate a recovery and nowhere to run a comparison — and creating such a place requires editing source rather than changing configuration.

**Evidence.** The build creates exactly one library, and creates it as a production library: `CRTLIB LIB(LIFE400) TYPE(*PROD)` [README.md:L125]. Every one of the twenty-six commands that compile or bind a program qualifies both its object and its source file with that same literal [README.md:L160-L176], [README.md:L182-L192], as do the four supporting object creations [README.md:L199-L206] and the scheduler entry [README.md:L209-L211]. The literal is equally fixed in the orchestration layer: the session entry program calls the menu by qualified name [QCLSRC/STRTLIFE.clle:L37]; the nightly job's two file [overrides](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#override) name it on both sides [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L60-L61]; and each of the three submitters names it in its overrides and in all four object names on its [`SBMJOB`](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#sbmjob) — the [job description](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#job-description), the [job queue](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#job-queue), the output queue and the message queue [QCLSRC/RUNSVC.clle:L44-L45], [QCLSRC/RUNSVC.clle:L50-L53], with the same shape in [QCLSRC/RUNNBUW.clle:L37-L38], [QCLSRC/RUNNBUW.clle:L43-L46] and [QCLSRC/RUNCLM.clle:L44-L45], [QCLSRC/RUNCLM.clle:L50-L53]. The complete configuration surface, and the fact that a submitted job resolves its unqualified file names against a library list no member of the estate sets, are owned by [the operational model](../current-state/06-operational-model.md) and are not re-derived here.

One property of name resolution matters to recovery specifically. The application library is added to the library list at the start of an interactive session and removed at the end [QCLSRC/STRTLIFE.clle:L27], [QCLSRC/STRTLIFE.clle:L40], so which objects a program actually reaches is a property of the job it runs in rather than of the program itself. Two copies of this application on one machine would therefore be told apart by whichever library a given job's list resolved first — a distinction carried by session state rather than by anything reviewable in the source.

**Consequence for recovery.** This is the finding with the most immediate consequence for the plan, and it has three parts. A restore cannot be rehearsed, because there is no non-production environment to restore into, so the recovery source that `CR-04` shows to be undefined could not be validated even if it were defined. A migration stage cannot be verified against comparable data before it goes live, because **a parallel run needs an environment that does not exist today**, and standing one up means editing the library literal in every CL member that carries it [QCLSRC/STRTLIFE.clle:L27], [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L60-L61], [QCLSRC/RUNNBUW.clle:L37-L38], [QCLSRC/RUNSVC.clle:L44-L45], [QCLSRC/RUNCLM.clle:L44-L45] and recompiling them — which is a change to the estate this assessment does not make and would itself need the safety net that `CR-01` through `CR-04` show to be absent. And any repair performed under `CR-07` is performed directly against production, because there is no other copy to try it on first.

**What the migration must therefore provide.** Environment separation as a prerequisite rather than a later convenience, with the environment identity supplied as externalized configuration instead of compiled into source, so that a second instance is a deployment rather than a source change. The externalization requirement itself is owned by [the target architecture](../target-state/01-target-architecture.md); the comparison this environment exists to support will be designed by the planned [the parallel run and output parity document](../migration/06-parallel-run-and-output-parity.md); and the constraint is offered as a premise for the planned [MOD-ADR-002](../decisions/MOD-ADR-002-migration-pattern.md).

## CR-07 — Reconciliation is necessarily manual, and the estate provides no tool for it

**Finding.** Because there is no unit of work, no change history, no established restore source and two two-file write paths whose residues differ, reconciliation after any failure is necessarily a manual exercise. The estate provides no means of performing it: it cannot scan a file, it cannot report on one, and its schema will not surface an inconsistency for it.

**Evidence — nothing in the estate can scan a data set.** Searching the code lines of all eight ILE COBOL members for `START` and for `READ NEXT` returns **zero occurrences of each**; the same census finds no `SORT` and no `MERGE`, and the result is recorded independently by [the current-state architecture](../current-state/02-architecture-current-state.md). Every one of the eleven database-file declarations in the estate specifies keyed random access, and not one specifies sequential access — the interactive servicing program's two are typical [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L38-L49] — so under the [record-level I/O](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#record-level-io) this estate uses throughout, the only way any program in LIFE400 can reach a record is by already knowing its key. **A scan for records that should not exist is therefore not expressible in the current design**, and neither is a scan for records that should exist and do not. The nightly job's own comments concede the same limitation from the other direction, observing that a fuller implementation would need a separate driver program to read the policy master sequentially [QCLSRC/DLYUPD.clle:L65-L69].

**Evidence — nothing in the estate can report on one either.** The two printer files hold complete report layouts [QDDSSRC/POLRPT.prtf:L15-L64], [QDDSSRC/CLMRPT.prtf:L15-L58] and are built by the documented build sequence [README.md:L153-L154], but no program in the estate references either of them, and the menu option that would reach a report reports its own absence instead [QCBLLESRC/MAINMENU.cbl:L81]. There is consequently no listing, no exception report and no output of any kind from which a reconciler could work — the disposition of all three of those facts belongs to [the known defects and stubs register](../current-state/07-known-defects-and-stubs.md).

**Evidence — the schema will not surface the inconsistency.** Detection cannot fall back on the data definitions, because the definitions declare nothing that a broken relationship would violate. No DDS member declares uniqueness on any key, and neither secondary file declares any relationship to the policy master, so a duplicate key and an orphaned child record are both possible by construction rather than prevented. The declaration-level inventory of those absences — the three keyed access paths, the two undeclared policy-identifier columns and the absence of any null state — is owned by [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md) and is referenced rather than restated. The consequence for this document is narrow and sufficient: **an orphaned servicing or claim record produces no constraint violation, so it is not detectable except by a comparison that nothing in the estate can perform.**

**Evidence — and the records themselves would not identify what went wrong.** Three properties of the schema limit what a manual reconciliation could conclude even with unrestricted access to the files. The policy master provides exactly two attribution columns — a last-maintenance date [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L75] and a last-action user [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L78] — and each holds a single value that every subsequent change overwrites, so neither carries a history. The date is eight digits with no time of day [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L75], so two changes made to the same policy on the same date cannot be ordered or even distinguished. And what actually reaches those two columns is `SEC-06`'s subject, while the fact that the interactive servicing path sets neither, and that the servicing file's own requesting-user column [QDDSSRC/SVCPF.pf:L51] has no writer, is `SEC-07`'s; this document re-argues neither. The consequence for a reconciler is what matters here: examining a suspect policy would not establish who changed it, in what order same-day changes occurred, or which of the two writes in a `CR-01` pair completed.

**Consequence for recovery.** Reconciliation is manual, is performed directly against the production library, has no report and no scan to work from, gets no help from the schema, and cannot determine attribution from the records it examines. Its scope is therefore unbounded in the only sense that matters operationally: nothing tells a reconciler which keys to examine, so the honest scope of any reconciliation over this estate is every record in the affected file.

**What the migration must therefore provide.** Read together, `CR-01` through `CR-07` reduce to five capabilities the target needs, and these are requirements rather than a procedure — the procedure that uses them will be written by the planned [cutover and rollback document](../migration/07-cutover-and-rollback.md).

- A real transaction boundary around every multi-file mutation, so that a failed operation leaves no residue to reconcile.
- A durable change history independent of the current row, so that a prior state can be reconstructed and an incident examined after the fact.
- A defined, scoped and exercised recovery source, so that restoring is a tested operation rather than an assumption.
- A non-production environment, so that a restore, a repair and a comparison can each be rehearsed before being performed on live data.
- A reconciliation capability that runs over the whole data set — enforced uniqueness and referential integrity so that inconsistency is prevented where it can be, and set-based queries and reporting so that it is detectable where it cannot.

That is the continuity half of the case for the migration pattern to be recorded in the planned [MOD-ADR-002](../decisions/MOD-ADR-002-migration-pattern.md): a programme that cut over in one step would be reversing itself with none of the five in place, whereas an incremental programme can build each one ahead of the stage that first needs it.
