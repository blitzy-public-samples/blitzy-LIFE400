# Compliance and Data Protection

This document inventories the personal and health-related data the LIFE400 estate holds, column by column, and records the retention, erasure, minimization and auditability gaps around it. It is the second document of the risk layer, which answers the first of the two business drivers this assessment was asked to serve — minimize security risk — and it exists because a driver stated that way cannot be discharged until somebody has written down *which* data is at stake and *where* it lives. **This document asserts no regulatory framework as applicable, in either direction.** It names no statute, regulation or standard as governing this system, maps no gap to any framework's article, clause or control number, and describes the estate as neither compliant nor non-compliant with anything. Framework applicability is a determination for the business, not for a source-code assessment, and the reason that boundary is drawn here rather than quietly crossed is set out under [framework applicability](#framework-applicability-must-be-confirmed-by-the-business) below.

**Scope.** This document owns four things: the column-level inventory of personal and health-related data, the retention exposure around it, the erasure and minimization gaps, and the compliance reading of the estate's inability to attribute anything to a person. It owns nothing else, and each boundary is another document's subject. It does **not** define the security finding set: `SEC-01` through `SEC-08` are established in [the security risk register](01-security-risk-register.md), and where this document needs one it references the identifier rather than re-arguing the evidence. It does not own the schema — column names, declared types, record lengths and the persisted-versus-transient classification belong to [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md), whose column names and anchors are reused here rather than re-derived. It does not analyse write atomicity, journaling, backup or recovery, which belong to [the continuity and recovery risk document](03-continuity-and-recovery-risk.md). It designs no control: naming the control that closes a gap is not the same as building one, and every control named below is designed in [the target security control design](../target-state/04-security-control-design.md). And it assigns **no** migrate, implement or drop verb to anything — every disposition in this assessment is the sole business of [the known defects and stubs register](../current-state/07-known-defects-and-stubs.md), which routes the retention consequence of unbounded file growth to this document precisely so that the exposure and the decision stay in separate places.

**No remediation is applied or proposed as work here.** This assessment plans a modernization; it does not perform one. No source member, [DDS](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#dds-data-description-specifications) member or build artifact is modified anywhere in it, and this document in particular contains no purge routine, no retention schedule definition, no data-definition statement and no masking or redaction logic. Gaps are documented and closing controls are named; nothing is implemented.

**Reading the citations.** A citation of the form `[<path>:<locator>]` is plain text rather than a hyperlink and points at a path in this repository. Plain text is chosen so that a citation stays meaningful in a diff and does not depend on a hosting provider's line-anchor syntax. **Every row of the inventory below carries its own column-level line citation**, because a sensitivity classification without an anchor is an opinion rather than a finding. Every line range was opened and read against the working tree while writing this document; none is carried forward from a prior description. Where a claim rests on an *absence* rather than on a line, the search that establishes the absence is stated so that it can be repeated. Platform terminology links to [the IBM i glossary](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md) on first use in this document.

**On the terms "PII" and "PHI", used descriptively and not as a framework claim.** Both appear below as ordinary descriptive labels — personally identifiable information, and personal health information — because they are the shortest accurate names for the two categories of data this estate holds. Neither is used in the technical sense any particular framework gives it, and using them is not an assertion that any framework's definitions reach this system. Where the distinction between a descriptive label and a legal characterisation matters, the descriptive label is the only one offered.

**What could not be verified, stated once and up front.** No claim below was validated by building or running anything, and none could be. No command available off the platform can compile, bind or execute this estate, and there is no test suite in the repository to run in its place. Every finding here therefore rests either on a citation a reader can resolve or on a search a reader can repeat, and nothing in this document should be read as having been confirmed by execution. The specific consequence for a data-protection analysis is worth naming: the *content* of a column can only be inferred from its declaration and its declared purpose, never observed, and where that inference cannot be drawn safely this document says so instead of guessing.

## What this document adds beyond SEC-04

`SEC-04` in [the security risk register](01-security-risk-register.md) records that personal and health data is stored in clear with no protection mechanism anywhere, and names four columns to establish the finding. It then defers the inventory here deliberately, and the reason that hand-off matters is arithmetic: **the finding is established by four columns, but the exposure is not four columns wide.**

Working outward from the declarations themselves, this document identifies **27 columns** across the three [physical files](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#physical-file) that hold personal, health-related, risk-derived, financial or attribution data — fifteen in the policy master, eleven in the claims file and one in the servicing file. That figure is derived here, column by column, against the persisted schema whose totals are owned and published by [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md); it is a classification of that schema rather than a second count of it. Three findings in this document exist nowhere in the register:

- **Underwriting-derived health and lifestyle attributes are persisted for the life of the record.** Smoking status, occupation class, underwriting class, high-risk avocation and a flat-extra rate loading are all stored on the policy master. `SEC-04` names none of them.
- **The claims file holds personal data about third parties who never transacted with the system**, in the form of a beneficiary name and that beneficiary's stated relationship to the insured.
- **There is no read-audit mechanism of any kind**, so a sensitive column can be read and displayed without leaving a trace. The register's auditability findings concern writes; this one concerns reads and is established under [the auditability gap](#auditability-gap) below.

## The personal and health data inventory

### How a row is classified

Each row states what the column **demonstrably contains**, evidenced by its DDS `TEXT` keyword and its position in the record, and nothing beyond that. Three disciplines govern the classification, and each exists because its opposite is the easy mistake:

- **A flag that records the receipt of a document is classified as a flag, not as the document.** `MEDRECS` is a medical-records *indicator*: it evidences that a medical file on this individual exists somewhere, which is itself sensitive, but it does not contain medical records and is not described as though it does.
- **A rating loading is classified as a rating loading.** `FLTXTRA` is a premium loading that serves as a proxy for an adverse health or risk finding. It is not a diagnosis and is not presented as one.
- **Where a category cannot be determined from the schema, the row says so.** One column in this estate is unstructured free text, and no honest classification of its contents can be made from DDS at all. It is marked as requiring business confirmation rather than assigned a category on the strength of its name.

No numeric sensitivity score and no severity band appears anywhere below. `SEC-03` establishes that object authority is undeclared, so who can actually reach these columns is a property of the installation and is not readable from this repository; and a severity rating needs inputs — asset value, threat model, exposure, installed configuration — that this repository does not contain. The categories used are the ones [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md) established for the columns it classified, extended here to the columns it did not: identifying personal data, health-related data, derived risk assessment, demographic, financial, identity-document receipt, payment instrument, unstructured free text, and attribution.

### POLMST — identity, health, demographic and risk-derived columns

The policy master is a physical file holding one record per policy, and the insured's identity and underwriting profile sit in plain character and [zoned decimal](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#zoned-decimal) columns within it. Nine columns carry personal, health-related, demographic or risk-derived content.

| File | Column | DDS type | Line citation | Data category | Why it is sensitive | Control gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `POLMST` | `INSNAME` | `40A` | [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L32] | Identifying personal data | The insured's name in clear characters — the primary identifier of a natural person, and the join key a reader needs to attach every other column below to a human being | Clear text, no encryption construct exists (`SEC-04`); no field-level access control; no read audit |
| `POLMST` | `INSDOB` | `8S 0` | [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L35] | Identifying personal data | Date of birth as an eight-digit `YYYYMMDD` integer — a strong identifier that, with a name, is sufficient to single out an individual and is not reissuable if exposed | Clear numeric, no encryption; no field-level access control; no read audit |
| `POLMST` | `ISSAGE` | `3S 0` | [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L37] | Demographic | Age at issue. Read against the issue date it narrows the date of birth independently, so it remains an identifying attribute even where the birth date itself is protected | Clear numeric; not recognised as identifying anywhere in the estate, so no control would be scoped to it |
| `POLMST` | `GENDER` | `1A` | [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L39] | Demographic | A recorded sex or gender attribute, used in rating. | `POLMST` | `OCCLAS` | `1S 0` | [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L43] | Derived risk assessment | Occupation class on a one-to-four scale. An occupational risk grading of a named person, derived at underwriting and never revisited | Clear numeric; no purpose-scoped retention; no read audit |
| `POLMST` | `UWCLAS` | `2A` | [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L45] | Derived risk assessment | Underwriting class. A non-zero value is a rating loading, and a loading is a proxy for an adverse health or risk finding about the insured. It is not a diagnosis, but it discloses that one was reached | Clear numeric; the inference it carries is not recognised as sensitive anywhere in the estate, so no control is scoped to it |

Two properties of this group matter more than any single row. The first is that **five of the nine are underwriting-derived** — the smoking status, the two class gradings, the avocation indicator and the rate loading — which means they were captured to reach a decision and are then retained for the whole life of the record with no distinction drawn between the two purposes. That is the substance of [the minimization gap](#minimization-gap) below. The second is that all nine sit in the same [record format](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#record-format) as the operational columns of the policy, `POLMSTREC` [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L14], with no structural separation of any kind: any path that opens the file for the plan code or the premium has the insured's name, date of birth and underwriting class in the same record area.

### POLMST — financial columns

Five monetary columns describe an identified individual's financial position with the insurer. They are separated from the group above because their sensitivity is of a different kind — they are not health or identity data, but they are not neutral either.

| File | Column | DDS type | Line citation | Data category | Why it is sensitive | Control gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `POLMST` | `SUMASSR` | `15S 2` | [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L52] | Financial | Sum assured — the death benefit contracted for a named individual, and the figure a beneficiary stands to receive | Clear numeric; no field-level access control; no read audit |
| `POLMST` | `LOANBAL` | `15S 2` | [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L54] | Financial | Policy loan balance. A non-zero balance discloses that the individual has borrowed against the contract, which is an inference about their financial circumstances | Clear numeric; no field-level access control; no read audit |
| `POLMST` | `ANPREM` | `15S 2` | [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L59] | Financial | Total annual premium. Read against the sum assured and the issue age it discloses the rating outcome, and therefore the risk assessment, without reading the underwriting columns at all | Clear numeric; no field-level access control; no read audit |
| `POLMST` | `MODPREM` | `15S 2` | [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L61] | Financial | Modal premium — the amount actually billed to the individual each billing period | Clear numeric; no field-level access control; no read audit |
| `POLMST` | `OUTPREM` | `15S 2` | [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L63] | Financial | Outstanding premium. A non-zero value discloses that the individual is behind on payments, which is an adverse financial inference about a named person | Clear numeric; no field-level access control; no read audit |

The inference chain in the third row is the one worth carrying forward into a control design. Because the annual premium, the sum assured and the issue age are all in the same record, **the underwriting outcome is reconstructible from the financial columns alone.** Protecting `UWCLAS` and `FLTXTRA` while leaving the premium columns open would therefore not protect the judgement they encode, which is why a control scoped column by column has to be designed against the inferences and not only against the labels.

### The audit column, and the personal data that reaches it

`LSTUSR 10A TEXT('LAST ACTION USER')` [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L78] is the policy master's only attribution column, and it does not attribute. `SEC-06` and `SEC-07` in [the security risk register](01-security-risk-register.md) establish why, and the evidence is not repeated here.

What belongs to *this* document is the data-protection consequence, and it is not the one a reader would expect from an audit column. `SEC-06` establishes — on the byte-offset analysis owned by [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md) — that the ten bytes reaching this column are the leading characters of the insured's name. So the column is not merely unattributed:

| File | Column | DDS type | Line citation | Data category | Why it is sensitive | Control gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `POLMST` | `LSTUSR` | `10A` | [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L78] | Attribution — in practice, identifying personal data | Its declared purpose is a user identifier, but per `SEC-06` the bytes that reach it are the leading characters of the insured's name, so the column holds identifying personal data under a name that describes something else entirely | Personal data in a column no classification exercise would examine; and no attribution, so it discharges neither purpose |

The consequence for a data-protection design is specific and easy to miss. **A classification exercise driven by column names and declared purposes would pass over this column**, because a field called "last action user" reads as operational metadata rather than as personal data. Any inventory of personal data for this estate that is built from the schema's own vocabulary is therefore incomplete by exactly one column, and the only reason this one is in the table above is that `SEC-06` went looking at the byte positions rather than at the names.

### The second access path over the same data

`POLMSTL1` is a [logical file](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#logical-file) over the policy master that declares no columns of its own: it has one record format naming its physical file and one key, `R POLMSTREC PFILE(POLMST)` and `K POLID` [QDDSSRC/POLMSTL1.lf:L13-L14]. It therefore adds no column to this inventory and creates no new data category — but it is a second route to the same clear-text columns, so a control scoped to one access path rather than to the data itself would not cover it. The access-path analysis proper, including whether this member needs any target counterpart at all, is owned by [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md).

Three observations follow, and each changes what a control has to cover rather than merely restating a row.

- **The beneficiary columns are the sharpest consent and notice exposure in the estate.** A beneficiary is named by the policyholder, not by themselves. They do not sign on and they hold no record of their own, and the search that establishes it is stated so it can be repeated: a case-insensitive search for the term across all twenty-four members matches only within the claims domain — the two columns above, the claim screen's own literal [QDDSSRC/CLMDSPF.dspf:L57], the shared contract's claim group [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L156-L157], and the two claim programs — and matches nothing at all in `QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf`, `QDDSSRC/SVCPF.pf` or `QDDSSRC/POLMSTL1.lf`. A beneficiary therefore exists in this estate only as two columns on a claim, and only once the insured has died. A protection, notice or retention design built around "the policyholder" misses them entirely, and they are the party with the least ability to discover that their data is held at all.
- **Four columns are pointers to data the estate does not contain.** The three document-receipt flags and the payment mode each evidence that a document or an instrument exists — a death certificate, a claim form, identity proof, banking details — while holding none of it. The flags are low-sensitivity in themselves and high-consequence as evidence, because they establish that personal data about these individuals is held *somewhere outside this system* and that nothing in this repository says where. That is a gap this assessment can identify and cannot close, and it is recorded as a confirm-with-the-business item rather than as a finding about the estate.
- **The free-text column is the one honest unknown, and it is left as one.** `CLMHOLD` is the single column in the inventory whose data category cannot be established from the schema. [The current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md) reaches the same conclusion from the same declaration. Assigning it a category on the strength of its `TEXT` keyword would be a guess presented as a classification, and an accurately marked unknown is a stronger input to a control design than a confident error, because it identifies the question that has to be asked instead of pre-empting it.

### What the inventory establishes

- **27 columns hold personal, health-related, risk-derived, financial or attribution data** — fifteen in the policy master, eleven in the claims file, one in the servicing file. The persisted schema those 27 sit within is owned and counted by [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md); the classification above is this document's, the schema is that one's.
- **Not one of the 27 carries a protection keyword**, and none could be protected by the application in any case: the exhaustive search recorded under `SEC-01` finds no encryption, decryption, cipher or hashing construct anywhere in the twenty-four members of the estate. There is no key management because there are no keys.
- **The estate holds data about at least two classes of natural person** — the insured and the beneficiary — and only one of them ever interacts with the system.
- **Two of the 27 are attribution columns that attribute nothing**, and one of those two holds identifying personal data instead.
- **One of the 27 cannot be classified at all** from the schema, and is marked as such.

## Retention exposure

Retention in LIFE400 is **indefinite by omission, not by policy.** That distinction is the whole of this section, and it rests on two independent absences that happen to compound. They are kept separate below because they are separate omissions with separate closing controls, and because collapsing them into a single "retention problem" loses the fact that fixing either one alone would leave the other intact.

**No intent is attributed anywhere in this section.** The source establishes what the estate does and does not do. It cannot establish whether anyone decided to retain data indefinitely, and this document does not characterise the outcome as a retention decision, a policy choice or an oversight — only as the consequence of two things that are absent.

### Omission one: no deletion operation exists

A census of all eight ILE COBOL members finds **zero `DELETE` operations**. The measurement is a per-member count over [QCBLLESRC/MAINMENU.cbl:L1-L95], [QCBLLESRC/NBUWMNT.cbl:L1-L498], [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L1-L507], [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L1-L448], [QCBLLESRC/SVCBILB.cbl:L1-L543], [QCBLLESRC/CLMMNT.cbl:L1-L291], [QCBLLESRC/CLMADJB.cbl:L1-L314] and [QCBLLESRC/POLMSTINQ.cbl:L1-L131], and it returns zero for every one of them. The same census is co-established as part of the absent-construct inventory in [the current-state architecture](../current-state/02-architecture-current-state.md#what-is-absent), which reaches the identical result and routes the retention consequence here.

The finding is broader than the deletion verb, and the wider search is worth stating because it forecloses the obvious alternatives. Searching all twenty-four members — the eight COBOL programs, the five ILE CL programs, the shared [copybook](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#copybook) and the ten DDS members — for `PURGE`, `ARCHIVE`, `ANONYM`, `PSEUDONYM`, `REDACT`, `MASK`, `RETENTION` and `ERASE` returns **zero occurrences of each**. So there is no deletion path, and there is also no archival path, no anonymization path, no pseudonymization path and no retention construct of any kind anywhere in the estate. The only file-changing verbs any member issues are `WRITE` and `REWRITE`, of which the servicing rewrite and its companion write are the clearest instance [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L190-L191].

### Omission two: no file declares a capacity

Separately and for unrelated reasons, nothing bounds how large these files may grow. Not one of the four file-creation commands in the documented build specifies a size: the three physical files [README.md:L140-L142] and the logical file [README.md:L143] are created without one, and `grep -c 'SIZE(' README.md` returns **0** for the whole document. No size keyword appears in any DDS member either. Growth is therefore bounded by nothing the estate declares. The configuration surface this belongs to is owned by [the operational model](../current-state/06-operational-model.md), and the disposition of the omission is owned by [the known defects and stubs register](../current-state/07-known-defects-and-stubs.md).

### The compound consequence

Read together, the two omissions fix the retention position structurally rather than by anyone's choice: **sensitive personal and health-related data accumulates without limit and without any mechanism to remove it.** Each omission alone would be materially less severe. An unbounded file with a working purge path is an operational capacity question. A file with no purge path but a declared ceiling at least forces the question when the ceiling is reached. Together they produce a store that grows for as long as it exists and has no exit for any record in it — and the data doing the accumulating is the 27 columns inventoried above, including a coded cause of death for every insured who has died and a name for every beneficiary who was ever paid.

Two further points bound what this section claims. **No data-volume figure appears here**, because none exists anywhere in this repository to cite and inventing one would be a fabricated measurement. And the retention position is a property of the *application*: whether an installation has arranged a platform-level save, retention or housekeeping regime around these files cannot be read from this repository in either direction, so neither the reassuring nor the alarming reading is offered. What is established is that **the delivered system contributes nothing to retention control and provides no route for anything else to do so through it.**

**Closing control.** A defined retention schedule with an implemented purge and archive path, applied per table and per data category, so that retention becomes a stated position with a mechanism behind it rather than the residue of two omissions. Designed in [the target security control design](../target-state/04-security-control-design.md), with the target structures it operates on in [the target data model and schema mapping](../target-state/03-target-data-model-and-schema-mapping.md).

## Erasure gap

There is no erasure path of any kind. The claim is stronger than the absence of a deletion verb, and each limb is separately established:

- **No deletion operation**, by the census under [omission one](#omission-one-no-deletion-operation-exists) above.
- **No soft-delete flag.** No column in any of the three physical files marks a record as deleted, withdrawn or suppressed. The status columns that exist — the contract status on the policy master [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L25], the claim status and decision on the claim record [QDDSSRC/CLMPF.pf:L43], [QDDSSRC/CLMPF.pf:L45] — describe where a business process has reached, not whether a record should still be held.
- **No archival or anonymization step**, by the wider search under [omission one](#omission-one-no-deletion-operation-exists).
- **No terminal status that removes anything.** This is the limb most easily mistaken for an erasure path, so it is stated precisely. The shared contract declares eight contract-status values [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L23-L30], among them terminated [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L29], claimed [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L28], lapsed [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L26] and declined [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L30]. Every one of them is a **value written onto a record that is retained in full.** A terminated contract is a policy record with two different characters in its status column and every one of its personal, health and financial columns still populated. Reaching the end of a contract's life in this estate removes nothing whatsoever. The transition census and the state machine those values form are owned by [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md).

**Consequence.** A request to erase an individual's data could not be satisfied by the system as built. There is no application operation to invoke, no flag to set that any code would honour, and no path that would leave the remaining records consistent. Satisfying such a request would require an out-of-band operation performed at the platform level, directly against the files, with **no application support, no referential safety and no audit of the operation itself** — the last point compounding with the auditability gap below, because a manual intervention against these files would leave no more trace than an ordinary read does. The absence is symmetrical and worth stating in one line: this estate can neither erase a record nor prove that it has not.

One boundary belongs here explicitly. Whether any individual is entitled to erasure, and on what basis, is a framework question this document does not answer — see [framework applicability](#framework-applicability-must-be-confirmed-by-the-business). What is established without reference to any framework is narrower and sufficient: **if such a request were made and the business decided it had to be honoured, the system as built could not honour it.**

**Closing control.** A defined retention schedule with an implemented purge and archive path, plus an auditable erasure operation that leaves both a consistent data set and a record that the erasure occurred. Designed in [the target security control design](../target-state/04-security-control-design.md); the mechanics of moving data into structures that can support it belong to [the data migration runbook](../migration/04-data-migration-runbook.md).

## Minimization gap

The estate persists underwriting-derived health and lifestyle attributes for the whole life of the record, and draws **no field-level distinction between data needed to administer the contract and data needed only at the underwriting decision point.** The columns are the ones inventoried above: smoking status [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L41], occupation class [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L43], underwriting class [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L45], high-risk avocation [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L47] and the flat extra rate [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L49]. Each was captured to reach an issue decision; each is then carried in the policy master for as long as the record exists, which — given the retention position above — is indefinitely.

The structural point is that the schema offers nowhere else for them to go. All five sit in `POLMSTREC` [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L14] alongside the plan code, the premium and the paid-to date, so there is no separation between decision-time data and administration-time data to draw a retention boundary along. A minimization control would have to introduce that boundary, not enforce an existing one.

### The one structural counterexample, and what it does not prove

One category of data in this estate is genuinely not persisted, and it is worth recording accurately because it is easy to mistake for a control. The shared contract declares a five-occurrence rider table, `PM-RIDER-TABLE OCCURS 5 TIMES` with its subordinate code, sum assured, rate, premium and status items and their condition names [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L88-L96], and **no DDS member declares any rider column at all.** Rider detail is therefore computed, validated and displayed, and then discarded with the record area.

**This is not a minimization control.** It is an incidental non-persistence arising from a gap between the shared contract and the schema — the data is not retained because there is nowhere to put it, not because anyone decided it should not be kept. Three things follow, and the distinction matters for each. A control that does not exist cannot be relied upon, so nothing about the target may be justified by reference to it. The behaviour is a defect from a functional standpoint even though its side effect is favourable from a data-protection standpoint, and those two readings must not be allowed to cancel each other out. And because the non-persistence is accidental, **giving riders first-class persistence in the target — which the rider-persistence decision does — increases the volume of retained data**, so the minimization control has to be designed to cover them from the outset rather than retrofitted afterwards. The rider analysis proper is owned by [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md), the disposition by [the known defects and stubs register](../current-state/07-known-defects-and-stubs.md), and the forward decision is recorded in [MOD-ADR-009](../decisions/MOD-ADR-009-rider-persistence.md).

**Closing control.** Purpose-scoped field retention, with an explicit justification recorded per sensitive column and a retention boundary that separates decision-time data from administration-time data. Designed in [the target security control design](../target-state/04-security-control-design.md), operating over the structures defined in [the target data model and schema mapping](../target-state/03-target-data-model-and-schema-mapping.md).

## Auditability gap

`SEC-06` and `SEC-07` in [the security risk register](01-security-risk-register.md) establish the write-side evidence: attribution names a program rather than a person, is timestamped with a batch process date rather than an event time, does not reach the audit column at all, and is absent entirely on the estate's dedicated amendment path. That evidence is not repeated here. What belongs to this document is the **compliance consequence** of it, and one finding the register does not cover.

**The compliance consequence.** No processing of personal data in LIFE400 is attributable to a person. Three things follow directly, and none of them is recoverable from anything the system stores:

- **There is no access log**, so no record exists of who reached the personal, health or financial columns inventoried above.
- **There is no basis for a record of processing activities** derived from the system itself. Such a record would have to be assembled from documentation and interview, because the estate holds no processing metadata — the one column that could have carried any is `LSTUSR` [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L78], and it holds insured-name text instead.
- **There is no way to demonstrate who read or changed a sensitive column**, which means that a suspected exposure could not afterwards be scoped, and an assertion that a column was *not* accessed could not be supported either. The asymmetry is the point: the absence of a log is not neutral, because it removes the ability to exonerate as well as the ability to detect.

### The read side, which the register does not cover

The register's auditability findings concern writes. **The estate contains no read-audit mechanism at all**, and the inquiry program demonstrates it end to end in a hundred and thirty-one lines.

`POLMSTINQ` declares the policy master as an indexed database file read by key [QCBLLESRC/POLMSTINQ.cbl:L38-L42] and opens it for input only [QCBLLESRC/POLMSTINQ.cbl:L63]. It reads a record by policy identifier, `READ POLMST KEY IS PM-POLICY-ID` [QCBLLESRC/POLMSTINQ.cbl:L102] — and both outcome branches of that read are literally `CONTINUE` [QCBLLESRC/POLMSTINQ.cbl:L103-L106], so nothing whatever is recorded about either a successful or an unsuccessful lookup. It then renders the record to the screen, including the insured's name in clear, `MOVE PM-INSURED-NAME TO SVCINSNAM` [QCBLLESRC/POLMSTINQ.cbl:L118]. Every `WRITE` statement in the program targets the [display file](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#display-file) rather than a database file [QCBLLESRC/POLMSTINQ.cbl:L125-L126], and because the policy master is opened for input the program could not write an audit record even if it were asked to.

**So a user can retrieve and view an insured's name, age, sum assured, premium and dates, one policy after another, and leave nothing behind.** The exposure is not a defect in the inquiry program — it is reading a file it opened for reading, which is exactly what it was built to do. It is the absence of any read-audit mechanism for it to participate in, and it compounds with `SEC-02`: because every signed-on user reaches every function, there is no population of users for whom this read path is out of bounds, and no record of which of them used it.

**Closing control.** User-attributed audit records covering **reads as well as writes**, carrying an authenticated principal and a real event timestamp, written to a column the writing path demonstrably populates. The read half is stated explicitly because an audit design scoped only to mutations would close `SEC-06` and `SEC-07` and leave this finding entirely open. Designed in [the target security control design](../target-state/04-security-control-design.md), on the identity model recorded in [MOD-ADR-005](../decisions/MOD-ADR-005-authentication-and-authorization.md).

## Framework applicability must be confirmed by the business

**This document asserts no regulatory framework as applicable, in either direction.** It names none as governing this system, maps no gap to any framework's article, clause or control number, and characterises the estate as neither compliant nor non-compliant with anything. The position is not an omission and not caution for its own sake — it is the resolution recorded as **A-03** in [the business drivers and success criteria document](../01-business-drivers-and-success-criteria.md#ambiguity-register), which this document consumes rather than re-derives. The requirement this assessment answers named no framework, and the inputs that would decide applicability — the jurisdictions the business operates in, the lines of business written, the contractual commitments made to policyholders and reinsurers, and the categories of individual whose data is held — are business facts that appear nowhere in this repository. Deriving applicability from source code would be an invention presented as a finding, and this assessment does not produce those.

**Framework applicability must therefore be confirmed by the business.** This document deliberately stops short of that determination and supplies its input instead: the control gaps below are documented generically, so that whichever frameworks the business confirms can be mapped onto them without any of the analysis needing to be redone. Each heading is a control family rather than a framework requirement.

### PII and PHI handling

Personal and health-related data is held in clear character and numeric columns with no protection mechanism, across the 27 columns inventoried above. There is no encryption at rest, no field-level access control, no separation between sensitive and operational columns in any record format, and no classification of any column as sensitive anywhere in the estate. Data about third-party beneficiaries is held on the same terms as data about policyholders. One column's contents cannot be characterised from the schema at all. Established above; the security framing is `SEC-04`.

### Retention and erasure

Retention is indefinite by omission — no deletion operation exists in any program, and no file declares a capacity. There is no purge path, no archival path, no anonymization or pseudonymization step, no soft-delete mechanism, and no terminal status that removes anything. A request to erase an individual's data could not be satisfied by the system as built. Established under [retention exposure](#retention-exposure) and [the erasure gap](#erasure-gap).

### Auditability

No processing of personal data is attributable to a person. Write attribution names a program rather than a user and does not reach the column it targets; the estate's dedicated amendment path attributes nothing at all; and there is no read-audit mechanism of any kind, so sensitive data can be viewed without a trace. Established under [the auditability gap](#auditability-gap); the write-side findings are `SEC-06` and `SEC-07`.

### Access control

Every signed-on user reaches every function through an identical option set, with no role, permission or authority test in the application, and object authority is left undeclared by every creation command in the documented build. So the population able to read the columns inventoried above cannot be determined from this repository at all. Established as `SEC-01`, `SEC-02` and `SEC-03` in [the security risk register](01-security-risk-register.md); the identity and role model that closes them is recorded in [MOD-ADR-005](../decisions/MOD-ADR-005-authentication-and-authorization.md).

### Transport security

The estate declares no transport protection for its only interface, so personal and health data returned to a terminal is carried by a channel to which the application contributes no confidentiality or integrity guarantee, and what an installation may have arranged around that channel cannot be read from this repository in either direction. Established as `SEC-05` in [the security risk register](01-security-risk-register.md), which also owns the attribution discipline for the platform-level claims involved.

### The one control that is present, evidenced and dated

Against everything absent above, the estate carries exactly **one** verifiable, documented control activity — and it is worth stating plainly, both because the finding would be unbalanced without it and because *it being the only one* is itself the sharpest single line in the security case.

The repository records a **Y2K date-field remediation**. Its own overview states that all date fields use an eight-digit `YYYYMMDD` format and that the programs were reviewed [README.md:L258], and points at the annotations left in the members themselves [README.md:L259]. Those annotations are present and citable: the `*Y2K-REVIEWED 1998-11-14` marker appears in the shared contract beside the date items it certifies [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L174], and the DDS members carry the corresponding banner line, `MODIFIED: 1998-11-14 - Y2K DATE FIELD REVIEW`, at the head of each file [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L8], [QDDSSRC/CLMPF.pf:L8], [QDDSSRC/SVCPF.pf:L8]. This is a genuine control activity of the kind an assurance review looks for: a defined scope, an applied remediation, a per-member record that it was carried out, and a dated sign-off. The dates in this paragraph are quoted evidence drawn from the source, not a schedule, and no temporal plan is implied by them anywhere.

Two conclusions follow, and the second is the load-bearing one:

- **It worked, and it is still visible.** The eight-digit date representation it produced is the representation the schema still uses [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L35], [QDDSSRC/CLMPF.pf:L26], which is why the remediation can be confirmed from the artifacts rather than taken on trust. The onward handling of those date integers is owned by [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md).
- **It is the only one.** Every other control family on this page is a gap. A single documented, evidenced and signed-off control activity — addressing a risk that was universal, well publicised and externally driven — is the entire assurance record of a system that holds causes of death, dates of birth and beneficiary names in clear text with no attribution of who reads them. The estate demonstrably *can* be remediated to a documented standard when a risk is recognised, which removes the argument that its condition is inherent; and the number of times that has been recorded is one.
