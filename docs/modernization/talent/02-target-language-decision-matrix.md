# Target Language Decision Matrix

This document names the language and runtime the LIFE400 core is to be rewritten in, and the datastore that follows from that choice. It is the document that answers the second stated business driver, because that driver — acquire engineering talent more familiar with a modern language — cannot be satisfied while "modern language" remains the unresolved variable. A sibling document establishes the gap this argues from: [the skills inventory and gap analysis](01-skills-inventory-and-gap.md) records what the estate demands today and why the pool able to supply it is narrower than the COBOL pool generally. This document establishes what to demand instead. The separation matters, because the gap is measured against the estate while the answer is measured against the labour market, and the two rest on entirely different evidence.

**Scope.** It owns three things and nothing else: the hard filter a candidate must pass before its other merits are weighed, the weighted criteria and the matrix that ranks five candidates against them, and the resulting primary recommendation with its runner-up and the reasons the remaining candidates are set aside. It does **not** decide where the resulting system runs — hosting and the exit from the current hardware are to be deliberately deferred in the planned [MOD-ADR-007](../decisions/MOD-ADR-007-hardware-exit-deferred.md), and [the platform and support status document](../current-state/03-platform-and-support-status.md) sets out why conflating the two is a documented failure mode. It does **not** choose a user-interface component library or design system; none is specified anywhere in this assessment, and that selection is deferred as an open gap by [the UI modernization document](../target-state/05-ui-modernization.md). It does **not** perform the column-by-column type mapping, which belongs to [the target data model and schema mapping](../target-state/03-target-data-model-and-schema-mapping.md). And it names no supplier: conversion-tooling maturity is weighed here as a property of an ecosystem, whereas procuring a tool is a commercial decision the requirement does not authorise.

**The success criteria this document is measured against are owned elsewhere.** They are `SC-2.1` through `SC-2.9` in [the business drivers and success criteria](../01-business-drivers-and-success-criteria.md), which also fixes what "a modern mainstream language" is taken to mean for this assessment. Three of them are discharged here in particular: `SC-2.1`, that exactly one language and runtime is named and no other document names a different one; `SC-2.2`, that the claim of a deep enterprise labour pool rests on attributed third-party evidence rather than on assertion; and `SC-2.4`, that monetary arithmetic in the target is exact decimal at the precision the legacy system uses. The ambiguity register entry that pre-registers this outcome, `A-01`, is owned there as well. This document is the analysis behind that resolution and does not restate the register.

**Reading the citations.** A citation of the form `[<path>:<locator>]` is plain text rather than a hyperlink and points at a path in this repository. It stays meaningful in a diff and does not depend on a hosting provider's line-anchor syntax. Every range cited below was opened and read, and no source member was altered to produce this document. Platform terms link to [the IBM i glossary](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md) on first use here.

**Attribution of external figures.** This document depends on information that is not in this repository more heavily than any other in the set, because a labour market cannot be read out of source code. Every such figure below names its publisher in the same sentence that uses it. None is presented as a measurement of this organisation, none is an estimate or projection for this programme, and where a publisher states a range it is reproduced as a range. Several of the sources below are vendors with a commercial interest in the figures they publish; that is disclosed at the point of use rather than left for the reader to infer. No cost, effort, duration or staffing figure appears anywhere in this document.

**This document is analysis, not a decision record.** It produces the reasoning two records rest on — [MOD-ADR-001 on the target language and runtime](../decisions/MOD-ADR-001-target-language-and-runtime.md), which is written and carries the status Proposed, and [MOD-ADR-003 on the target datastore](../decisions/MOD-ADR-003-target-datastore.md), which is planned and not yet written — and it therefore carries no status of its own. If the recommendation is overturned, the record is superseded and this analysis becomes the evidence the superseding record argues against, which is why the rationale lives in one place rather than being restated in every document that depends on it.

**No programme schedule appears below.** No date, duration, ordering or calendar of work for this programme is stated or implied, and no cost, effort or staffing figure appears anywhere. Publication dates do appear, and are required: an external figure is checkable only if the reader knows which edition it came from, so every source in the external-sources section below carries the date its publisher put on it. Release cadence is described as the policy the publisher states — a long-term-support line, a published support window — rather than by version number, and no release number appears anywhere below, because the recommendation is a language and a support line rather than a version, for the reason given under [primary recommendation and runner-up](#primary-recommendation-and-runner-up). And no claim here rests on a build: nothing in this environment can compile, bind or run LIFE400, for the reasons [the platform and support status document](../current-state/03-platform-and-support-status.md) establishes.

## The hard filter: exact decimal arithmetic

A filter that eliminates candidates outright must be applied before anything is weighed. Presenting exact decimal arithmetic as one weighted row among many would be a logical error, because it would let a candidate accumulate enough points elsewhere to score its way past a correctness requirement. So it is applied first, on its own, and only the candidates it admits are eligible for the core. The weights that follow decide between those; they are shown for all five candidates so the margin is visible and the rejections are auditable rather than asserted.

### Why the precision is a business invariant rather than a type-system artifact

The strongest fact available is not that LIFE400 handles money. It is that the same precision is declared **twice, independently, in two different languages**, and the two declarations agree.

On the stored side, [DDS](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#dds-data-description-specifications) declares the monetary columns of the policy master as [zoned decimal](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#zoned-decimal) at fifteen digits with two decimals: the sum assured [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L52], the policy loan balance [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L54], the total annual premium [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L59], the modal premium [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L61] and the outstanding premium [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L63]. The `S` in `15S 2` is the DDS type letter for zoned decimal rather than a per-column sign annotation; what the platform's sign handling then does with those columns is a question [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md) owns and settles, and nothing in this document turns on it.

On the program side, the shared [copybook](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#copybook) declares the same quantities as thirteen integer digits plus two decimals — the same fifteen-digit, two-decimal precision written the other way. The premium-results group carries seven such items [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L98-L105], the sum assured is declared the same way [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L76], and so are the per-plan sum-assured bounds the underwriting rules test against [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L42-L43].

Two independent declarations of one precision, in two languages, maintained by different mechanisms, is the signature of a business invariant rather than of one file's type system. That is why the requirement survives the migration: whatever the target stores money in has to reproduce fifteen digits and two decimals exactly, because both sides of the legacy system already agree that is what money is here. The field-level enumeration of every column and every contract item, and the analysis of where the two sides diverge, belong to [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md) and are not re-derived here.

### The concrete stake: the one signed field

Exactly one item in the whole shared contract carries a leading `S` in its picture clause, and it is `PM-PREMIUM-DELTA` [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L106]. Among the contract's numeric items it is the sole one declared able to hold a negative value, and what it holds is the amendment repricing delta — the difference between a policy's new total annual premium and its old one.

That is not an abstract correctness concern, because the estate does three things with it:

- It computes the delta on the batch servicing path, in the repricing [paragraph](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#paragraph) `3100-REPRICE-POLICY`, as the new total annual premium minus the old [QCBLLESRC/SVCBILB.cbl:L428-L429].
- It computes the delta a second time on the online path, from the same two quantities [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L421-L422]. The two paths duplicating each other rather than sharing one implementation is a structural liability [the current-state architecture](../current-state/02-architecture-current-state.md) owns; what matters here is only that money arithmetic runs in both.
- It then puts the online delta on a screen, moving it into a display field in the result-display paragraph [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L442], and stores it in the servicing file [QDDSSRC/SVCPF.pf:L45].

So the single place this system is declared able to represent a negative amount [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L106] is the figure an operator reads back to a policyholder whose premium has just changed [QCBLLESRC/SVCMNT.cbl:L442]. A representation error there does not surface as a rounding artifact in a log. It surfaces as a disputed charge. One sentence is enough to make the requirement concrete, and this is it.

### What the arithmetic actually does

A storage type alone would not settle the question, because the numbers are computed rather than merely held. Across the eight [ILE](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#ile-integrated-language-environment) COBOL programs there are seventy-one `COMPUTE` statements, and the ones that produce premiums have a shape that is precisely where binary floating point diverges from exact decimal.

- The base annual premium divides the sum assured by one thousand and then multiplies the result by five separate four-decimal rating factors in a single expression [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L389-L395], with a second division by one thousand in the flat-extra adjustment that follows [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L397-L399].
- The tax amount multiplies a fifteen-digit, two-decimal money value by a four-decimal rate [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L443-L444], where the rate itself is declared with four decimal places [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L52], and the product is received by a field with two.
- The modal premium divides the total annual premium by a billing divisor — one, two, four or twelve — and then multiplies by a four-decimal loading factor [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L462-L464]. The online program repeats the identical expression [QCBLLESRC/NBUWMNT.cbl:L469-L471], and the batch servicing program computes the tax the same way again [QCBLLESRC/SVCBILB.cbl:L518-L519].

Division by twelve does not terminate in decimal, so every one of those expressions produces an intermediate value with more decimal places than the field receiving it. What happens next is the part that raises the bar on the target, and it is established by exhaustive search rather than inference: **no `COMPUTE` in the estate carries a `ROUNDED` phrase, and no arithmetic statement carries an `ON SIZE ERROR` clause.** A search of all eight COBOL programs, all five ILE [CL](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#cl-control-language) programs, the copybook and all ten DDS members returns no occurrence of either. Every result is therefore truncated to the receiving field's declared scale by language default, silently.

The consequence for language selection is sharper than "money needs decimals". The target needs an exact decimal facility in which **scale and rounding behaviour are explicit and controllable at each operation**, because reproducing this system's outputs means reproducing truncation at a declared scale, not applying whatever rounding a default supplies. Scale is not uniform either — a four-decimal flat extra rate is stored alongside two-decimal money [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L49], and the rating factors are four-decimal in the contract [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L85-L87] — so a single fixed money type is insufficient and a configurable-scale decimal type is required. The count of distinct scales and their file-side counterparts are owned by [the current data model](../current-state/04-data-model-current-state.md).

Why this matters at all rather than being a curiosity: output parity against the legacy system is the acceptance condition for the migration, and the parity comparison is field-level. The strategy that will make that comparison possible belongs to [the characterization test strategy](../migration/05-characterization-test-strategy.md), planned and not yet written, and the reason it is indispensable here is that the estate contains no test member of any kind against which a conversion could otherwise be checked.

### The rule that follows

The target must offer an exact decimal type for money, with explicit scale and explicit rounding, and must never carry a monetary value through binary floating point. The reason is not a textbook floating-point example: most of those agree once truncated to two decimals, and one that agrees proves nothing here. It is a computation this estate performs, with the constants the estate itself supplies.

The modal premium divides the total annual premium by a billing divisor and multiplies by a four-decimal loading factor [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L462-L464]. For semi-annual billing the divisor is `2` and the factor is `1.0150` [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L453-L454]; the result is received by a thirteen-digit, two-decimal field [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L104] and stored in a `15S 2` column [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L61]. Take a total annual premium of `100.00` and carry both paths to that receiving scale:

```text
exact decimal   100.00 / 2 = 50.00  x 1.0150 = 50.750000          -> at scale 2: 50.75
binary double   100.0  / 2 = 50.0   x 1.015  = 50.74999999999999  -> at scale 2: 50.74
                the double's value in full is 50.749999999999992894572642398998...
```

Both divisions are exact and both paths agree to fifteen significant digits. The divergence is produced entirely by the multiplication, and it survives the write because the receiving field truncates rather than rounds — a value a fraction of a cent below `50.75` becomes `50.74`, and there is no `ROUNDED` phrase anywhere in the estate to absorb it. One cent per instalment on one policy is enough, because the acceptance comparison is field-level. The monthly pair behaves the same way: a total annual premium of `401.00` divided by `12` and loaded by `1.0800` [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L459-L460] yields `36.09` on the exact path and `36.08` on the binary one.

The representation decision itself is to be recorded in [MOD-ADR-006 on date and decimal representation](../decisions/MOD-ADR-006-date-and-decimal-representation.md), a planned record, which will also carry the companion consequence that the estate's eight-digit `YYYYMMDD` date integers — the paid-to date [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L71] among them, and the whole date group of the shared contract [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L108-L115] — become real date types in the target. The date argument is not developed here; it belongs to that record and to [the target schema mapping](../target-state/03-target-data-model-and-schema-mapping.md).

### Applying the filter, candidate by candidate

Every one of the five candidates has *some* exact-decimal facility, so stating the filter as an availability test would be both factually weak and easy to refute. What it actually discriminates is narrower and more decisive. **In none of the five is the exact decimal type what arithmetic does by default** — every one of them defaults to a binary numeric type, and in every one of them using the exact type is a deliberate act. So the filter tests three other things: whether the exact decimal type is a first-class part of the language or its standard library rather than a dependency, whether the mainstream database-access layer maps a fixed-scale decimal column to it without being told to, and whether a lapse is loud — visible in the source, or failing at the operation — or silent.

| Candidate | Exact decimal comes from | Type discipline: what the engineer must do deliberately | Fixed-scale column maps to it by default? | Filter verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Java | The standard library, as an arbitrary-precision decimal type that carries its scale as part of the value and refuses an inexact division outright unless given a rounding mode | Naming the type at every monetary declaration, and constructing it from a string rather than from a binary literal. The language's own literals and operators are primitives, so nothing about this is automatic. What the language supplies is that the type is the conventional money representation and that an inexact division fails rather than rounding quietly, so a lapse in the arithmetic tends to surface as an exception rather than as a wrong figure | Yes — the standard database interface and the mainstream persistence layers map a decimal column to it without configuration | **Passes without conditions** |
| C#/.NET | The language itself, as a built-in decimal type with its own numeric literal form, tracking scale through arithmetic | Declaring the decimal type and writing its literal suffix. The unsuffixed literal is binary, so the choice is deliberate here too. What the language supplies is that the suffix makes the choice visible in the source, so an accidentally binary monetary value is a reviewable mistake rather than an invisible one | Yes — the mainstream data-access and object-relational layers map a decimal column to it without configuration | **Passes without conditions** |
| Python | The standard library, as a decimal type with an explicit context carrying both precision and rounding mode — the most explicit rounding control of the five | Constructing the decimal from a string, and keeping every arithmetic site and module boundary away from the built-in numeric type. The literal and every operator are binary floating point, and the interpreter distinguishes the two only at run time | Largely — the mainstream driver and object-relational layers return the decimal type for a fixed-scale column by default | **Passes only under a standing discipline** |
| TypeScript/Node | A third-party library; neither the language nor its standard library provides one, and the built-in arbitrary-precision integer type cannot carry a scale | Adopting and holding a dependency, and re-establishing exactness at every untyped library boundary. The sole built-in numeric type is binary floating point, and it is what every arithmetic operator produces | No — drivers commonly surface a fixed-scale column as a string or as a binary number, and preserving exactness requires deliberate configuration at each one | **Does not pass** |
| Go | A third-party library; the language has no decimal type and the standard database interface has none to scan into | Adopting a dependency and wiring its scanner per column. The built-in numeric types are binary integers and binary floats | No — scanning a fixed-scale column into a decimal requires the third-party library's own scanner, wired up per column | **Does not pass** |

The filter therefore narrows the field to two candidates that pass without conditions, admits a third only if a convention is maintained indefinitely by every engineer who touches the money paths, and excludes two. It does not say that a correct system cannot be built in the excluded languages — it can, with discipline and a dependency. Nor does it say that exactness is automatic in the two that pass: **in all five candidates, using the exact decimal type is a deliberate act, and in none of them is it what happens by default.** What the filter separates is narrower and checkable: whether the type is supplied by the language or its standard library rather than by a dependency, whether the persistence layer hands back the exact type without being configured to, and whether a lapse is loud — a visible literal form, or an arithmetic operation that fails rather than rounding quietly — or silent.

The consequence is that no candidate discharges the requirement on its own. Whichever is chosen, three things remain obligations of this programme rather than properties of the language: a written standard fixing the monetary type and the rounding mode at each operation, a review of every converted money path looking specifically for a reintroduced binary type, and a test asserting the type as well as the value. That obligation is carried as a named consequence of [MOD-ADR-001](../decisions/MOD-ADR-001-target-language-and-runtime.md) rather than left as an inference here. It matters most for a codebase whose acceptance test is field-level output parity and which arrives with no test member anywhere in the estate to catch a lapse — an absence established by the census in [the current-state architecture](../current-state/02-architecture-current-state.md#what-is-absent).

Every criterion below serves Driver 1 (minimize security risk), Driver 2 (acquire engineering talent more familiar with a modern mainstream language), or both. A criterion serving neither would not belong in a matrix answering these two drivers, however interesting it might be on its own, and none is included.

These three carry the heaviest weights because each is load-bearing for this estate specifically rather than for modernization in general.

### Scoring scale and the status of these weights

- Each candidate is scored on each criterion from `1` to `5`, where `1` means the criterion is poorly served, `3` means adequately served with a material caveat, and `5` means served without reservation. Integers only; no half-points, because a half-point would imply a precision the underlying judgement does not have.
- Weights sum to `100`, so a weighted total falls between `100` and `500` and the arithmetic can be checked by hand from the table.
- **The weights are a documented judgement, not a measurement.** Nothing in this repository determines that exact-decimal ecosystem maturity deserves twenty points and batch maturity six. What the repository determines is that both are relevant and which is more consequential for this estate; the numbers express that ordering so it can be argued with. A reader who disagrees can substitute their own and recompute, which is the point of publishing the weights rather than only the conclusion.
- The scores are judgements too, but of a different kind: each is defended in the candidate evaluation below, and the ones resting on external evidence name their publisher there.

Four properties of that result are worth stating plainly, including the two that cut against the conclusion.

- **The top two positions are insensitive to any single criterion.** Thirty-six points separate first from second, and that margin decomposes into exactly four criteria: `C1` contributes twenty, `C3` fifteen and `C7` six in favour of the leader, while `C9` contributes five against it. Removing any one of the ten criteria entirely and recomputing therefore leaves first place unchanged — the largest single reduction, removing `C1`, still leaves a sixteen-point margin. Second place is insensitive on the same test by a wider margin. The recommendation does not depend on one contested weight, which is the property that makes it defensible rather than merely arithmetic.
- **The gap between third, fourth and fifth is not a meaningful separation.** Thirty-two points separate third from fourth and ten points separate fourth from fifth, on a five-hundred-point scale. Those three candidates are best read as one group the filter had already placed outside the set that passes without conditions — one conditionally, two not at all — rather than as a ranked tail. Reporting the order without that caveat would overstate what the arithmetic supports.
- **The leading candidate scores worst of the five on developer desirability.** That is the honest cost of the recommendation and it is scored, not hidden. The reason it does not change the outcome is that `C9` carries five points while `C1` and `C3` carry thirty-five between them, and Driver 2 asks for a pool that can be hired from rather than for the language engineers most want to use. `C9` is also the criterion resting least on reproduced evidence: no candidate-level desirability figure from any survey is reproduced in this document, and the scores on it are declared judgements informed by which languages the published most-admired list names — none of the five among them.
- **Popularity is scored nowhere.** No criterion rewards a language for total usage share. That is deliberate: the ecosystem with the largest overall usage share is among the candidates the filter excludes, and a matrix that rewarded usage share would have surfaced it as a contender for a policy-administration core it is unsuited to.

## Candidate evaluation

Each candidate is assessed on its own terms and ends in a verdict. A matrix that strawmans the options it rejects is not auditable, so each subsection states what the candidate is genuinely good at before saying why it does or does not win this particular decision.

### Java

Java passes the filter without conditions. Its standard-library decimal type is the conventional representation for money, it carries its scale as part of the value rather than as a formatting concern, and it will not perform an inexact division at all unless the caller names a rounding mode. None of that is automatic, and the score does not claim it is: Java's own numeric literals and operators are primitives, the decimal type has to be named at every monetary declaration, and constructing one from a binary literal rather than from a string reintroduces precisely the error the type exists to prevent. What the language supplies is a conventional type, a scale that travels with the value, and a failure mode that is loud rather than silent. The last of those is unusual and directly useful here: the estate truncates silently at a declared scale in every premium calculation [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L443-L444], and a target that forces the truncation decision to be written down is a target in which that behaviour can be reproduced deliberately instead of inherited by accident.

On labour-pool depth the available evidence points at the right vertical, and it is adoption evidence rather than hiring evidence. OpenLogic's *State of Open Source Report* finds that of all industries it surveyed, the banking, insurance and financial-services sector reports the highest Java usage, closely followed by government and public services, and that Spring adoption is likewise highest in that same sector at 47.22 per cent; the publisher is a commercial provider of open-source support. Azul's *2026 State of Java Survey & Report*, published 10 February 2026 and administered by Dimensional Research across more than 2,000 Java professionals on five continents surveyed between September and November 2025, describes an ecosystem in active enterprise development rather than in maintenance — 62 per cent of the organisations it surveyed now write AI functionality in Java, up from 50 per cent the year before. Azul is a commercial Java runtime vendor and commissioned the research, and its own chief technology officer states the survey was fielded exclusively to Java users, so it cannot measure Java's standing against another language and no share-of-market claim is drawn from it here.

**What that evidence does not do is measure hireable supply**, and `C2` scores four rather than five for exactly that reason. An industry reporting the highest usage of a language is where the incumbent engineers are, which is a strong indicator of where a pool exists and a weak one for how quickly a role in a given market gets filled. No job-posting census or vacancy-fill measurement for insurance back-office work by language was located for this assessment. The honest position is that the proxy points consistently in one direction across two independent commercial publishers, and that confirming it in the organisation's own recruiting markets is work for the business rather than a conclusion of this document.

On conversion tooling Java has the broadest coverage of the five, and the score is four rather than five because the coverage is uneven across the five components `C3` is defined over. Taking them in turn, and naming only offerings that name IBM i or ILE rather than the mainframe:

| Component | What is published for a Java target | What is not |
|---|---|---|
| ILE COBOL procedural logic | SoftwareMining publishes a page specifically for iSeries ILE COBOL, describing its rule-based translator's output for Java or C#; Fresche Solutions publishes an IBM i modernization product transforming COBOL, RPG and Synon into Java; SoftRoad America publishes an IBM i converter naming COBOL, RPG and RPGLE to Java | Nothing verifiable about the fidelity of the output on an estate of this shape, and no published measurement of it |
| ILE CL orchestration | SoftRoad America's converter names `CLP` and `CLLE` to Java or shell — the only offering located that names CL at all | No second source, and nothing addressing monitored conditions, override scope or job submission semantics specifically |
| DDS persistence and keyed access | SoftwareMining describes translating DB400 keyed file definitions and record operations into object-relational modules reaching a relational database by SQL; Fresche publishes DDS-to-DDL database modernization; a DDS-to-SQL converter is described independently | The absent uniqueness, referential integrity and nullability of the legacy definition are design decisions no converter can make; they are owned by [the target schema mapping](../target-state/03-target-data-model-and-schema-mapping.md) |
| DDS 5250 presentation | SoftwareMining describes translating DDS screen layouts into server-rendered pages that retain the character-based appearance | A retained character-based appearance is a migration aid, not the target interface. Mapping the record formats to target screens is owned by [the UI modernization document](../target-state/05-ui-modernization.md), and no component library is named anywhere in this assessment |
| Operational reconstruction and the test net | Nothing | This is the largest uncovered component. No offering consulted reconstructs scheduling, submission or message handling, and none supplies the characterization suite the estate lacks entirely |

Two pieces of counter-evidence belong here rather than in a footnote, because omitting them would overstate the score. An independent IBM i practitioner writing on modernization options states that automated conversion tools for this platform family have been marketed for over two decades and that in practice they emit target-language code structured like the source language — difficult for engineers hired for the target language to maintain, which is the precise outcome Driver 2 exists to avoid. And IBM's own platform-specific code assistant for IBM i has, per trade reporting, been folded together with its mainframe COBOL-to-Java counterpart into a successor product, with the prior assistants described as largely uncommercialized — so IBM-supplied tooling for this conversion is best treated as in transition rather than as an established option.

Two widely cited figures are deliberately **not** used as evidence for this estate. Astadia's published range — that other providers in the mainframe-modernization market automate 70 to 80 per cent of a conversion with the remainder done by hand — is a vendor statement about competitors in the mainframe market, and is reproduced here only as an attributed range for that market, not as an expectation for ILE COBOL on IBM i. Amazon Web Services describes its transformation service as refactoring COBOL into Java Spring Boot applications, but that service targets mainframe workloads; it is evidence that the recommended stack is a conventional conversion target, and it is not evidence of IBM i coverage. Against all of that, the position of the other candidates is still weaker: one has a narrower version of the same coverage, and three have none.

Static typing and refactoring support are the strongest available, which matters more here than it would on a project with tests. Batch maturity is first-class: chunk-oriented, restartable batch processing with skip and retry semantics is a standard part of the ecosystem rather than a library to assemble, which is what replacing a nightly driver requires. Relational access maps a fixed-scale decimal column to the decimal type without configuration. Long-term-support lines are published with stated support windows, and multiple independent builds of the runtime exist, so patch supply does not depend on a single supplier — the direct counter to the exposure the current platform baseline creates [README.md:L264].

The honest cost is developer enthusiasm, and it is the one dimension Java loses on outright. The Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025 — more than 49,000 responses from 177 countries across 62 questions and 314 technologies, published 29 July 2025 — names Rust at 72 per cent, Gleam at 70 per cent, Elixir at 66 per cent and Zig at 64 per cent as the most-admired languages of that edition, and names none of the five candidates evaluated here. No candidate-level admiration or desirability percentage is reproduced from that survey in this document, so the score of two is a declared judgement informed by that list and by the relative signal below rather than a figure lifted from a table. The gap it represents is real, it is why `C9` exists as a criterion, and it is why Java scores two rather than five on it. A second caution belongs here and is a Driver 1 caution rather than a Driver 2 one: OpenLogic's report also finds that 33 per cent of banking, insurance and financial-services organisations still run a web-server version that no longer receives community support or security patches. Choosing a language with a healthy support line does not by itself deliver patch supply — the upgrade discipline has to be maintained, and this recommendation should not be read as making that problem go away.

**Verdict.** Passes the filter without conditions and ranks first. The margin is produced by the heaviest-weighted criteria rather than by the supporting ones, and within those by exact-decimal ergonomics and conversion breadth rather than by labour-pool depth, where it ties with the runner-up. Recommended for the core.

### C# and .NET

C#/.NET is a genuinely close second and should be read as a defensible alternative rather than as a foil. Its decimal type is built into the language with its own numeric literal form, which makes an accidentally binary monetary value a visible mistake in the source rather than a silent one — arguably a better ergonomic than the recommended candidate offers. That is an ergonomic and not an automatic guarantee: an unsuffixed numeric literal here is binary, so declaring the decimal type and writing its suffix is as deliberate an act as naming the type is in the recommended candidate. Its mainstream data-access and object-relational layers map a fixed-scale decimal column to it without configuration, so it scores level with Java on `C8`. Its long-term-support cadence is published with stated windows, and its security, dependency-scanning and static-analysis tooling is equally mature, so it scores level on `C5` and `C6` as well. Static typing and automated refactoring are level on `C4`. On developer sentiment it does better than the recommended candidate, not worse, and the score of three against two is a relative judgement rather than a measured difference: the TIOBE Index named C# its programming language of the year for 2025, an award that index publishes annually. That index measures search-engine query volume for a language, which is a proxy for attention and not a measurement of employment demand or of developer preference, and it is used here only to order two candidates on a five-point criterion carrying five of the hundred weights.

Three criteria account for the thirty-six-point difference in this matrix, and one of the three turns out not to separate the two candidates at all. None of them is a deficiency in the language. The remaining part of the margin is two criteria explained in the Java assessment above rather than repeated here — six points on batch maturity in Java's favour, and five points on developer desirability in C#'s.

- On `C1`, Java's decimal type refuses an inexact division without an explicit rounding mode, whereas the .NET decimal applies a default. Both preserve scale and both are exact; the difference is only that one of them forces the rounding decision to be written down. Given that reproducing silent truncation at a declared scale is the actual requirement here [QCBLLESRC/NBUWB.cbl:L462-L464], being compelled to state the rounding at every division is a small but real advantage on this estate. On a system without that constraint the two would score the same.
- On `C2`, the two candidates **tie at four**, and neither reaches five, because the criterion has no hiring measurement behind it for either of them. What separates them is only that the adoption proxy naming the insurance vertical is a Java report with no C# counterpart consulted here, so C#'s enterprise pool is evidenced as deep in general while its *insurance back-office* depth is not separately evidenced at all. That is a gap in the available evidence rather than a demonstrated deficiency in the pool, it does not cost C# a point in this matrix, and it is the criterion most likely to move for either candidate once the business tests its own recruiting markets.
- On `C3`, C# scores three against Java's four, and the difference is breadth across the five components rather than the existence of a translator. SoftwareMining's iSeries ILE COBOL page names C# alongside Java as a target, so the procedural-logic component is covered by a vendor that names this platform explicitly, and Astadia names C# as a target for its mainframe refactoring work. What has no C# counterpart in anything consulted is an offering naming ILE CL conversion, an IBM i database-definition modernization path, or an IBM i-specific product line at the depth published for Java. The uncovered fifth component — operational reconstruction and the test net — is uncovered for both.

**Verdict.** Passes the filter without conditions and ranks second. Recorded as the documented runner-up: if the primary recommendation is revisited, this is the candidate it should be revisited in favour of.

### TypeScript and Node

TypeScript is a serious language with excellent static typing, first-rate editor tooling and by far the largest adjacent pool of engineers. The Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025 reports JavaScript — the language TypeScript compiles to, and a distinct entry in that survey — as the most-used language of that edition at 66 per cent; no separate figure for TypeScript is reproduced here. That adjacency is the reason it scores five on onboarding accessibility, and its four on desirability is a declared judgement on the same footing as the other candidates' rather than a figure. None of that is in dispute.

It does not pass the filter, and the reason is structural rather than a matter of taste. The sole built-in numeric type is binary floating point, so it is what every arithmetic operator produces by default and what every untyped library boundary hands back. Exact decimal arithmetic is available only as a third-party dependency, and the language's arbitrary-precision integer type cannot carry a scale, so it does not substitute. That means correctness for the fifteen-digit, two-decimal monetary fields [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L52] would rest on every developer, and every library on every code path, remembering not to use the default. The database boundary makes it worse rather than better: drivers commonly surface a fixed-scale column as a string or as a binary number, so exactness has to be re-established deliberately at each one, which is why it scores two on `C8`. Batch maturity is the other relative weak point, and it scores three rather than two because the common statement of the objection is too strong. The runtime executes JavaScript on a single event loop, but CPU-bound and long-running work is routinely moved off it — worker threads, clustered processes and separate worker processes are all standard, and a batch sweep is in any case dominated by database round trips rather than by computation. The accurate objection is narrower: what the ecosystem does not supply as a first-class framework concern is the chunk-oriented, restartable, skip-and-retry batch semantics that replacing a nightly driver needs, so that scaffolding would be assembled rather than adopted. And there is no established COBOL-to-TypeScript conversion tooling in any catalogue consulted, which is why `C3` scores one.

The rejection must be scoped honestly, because it is narrower than it sounds. **It is a rejection for the core only.** Nothing here argues against TypeScript in a presentation tier, where no monetary arithmetic is performed and values arrive already computed and already rounded. That is a separate decision, it is not taken in this document, and it is deferred as an open gap by [the UI modernization document](../target-state/05-ui-modernization.md) — which also records that no component library or design system is specified anywhere in this assessment.

**Verdict.** Evaluated and rejected for the core. Not rejected for the presentation tier, where the decision is deferred and belongs to another document.

### Python

Python is the candidate it would be easiest to be unfair to, and the unfair version of the argument — that it cannot do exact decimals — is simply false. Its standard-library decimal type is excellent, and its explicit context carrying both precision and rounding mode is arguably the most direct rounding control of any candidate here, including the recommended one. Its mainstream driver and object-relational layers return that decimal type for a fixed-scale column by default, which is why it scores four on `C8` — better than two of the other candidates. It has a very large and growing labour pool; the Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025 reports its usage rising by seven percentage points year on year, and it scores five on desirability accordingly. Its published releases carry stated security-fix windows.

The reasons it does not take the core are three, and they are precise.

- **The default numeric type is binary floating point.** A decimal literal in Python source is a binary float, so exactness is a convention rather than a property of the language — the same structural issue as TypeScript, differing in degree because the exact type is in the standard library rather than in a dependency and because the persistence layer defaults the right way. That is why the filter verdict is conditional rather than a failure, and why `C1` scores three rather than one or five.
- **The type-checking safety net is opt-in rather than inherent, and that matters most here.** This is the decisive supporting reason, and it is specific to this estate rather than a general preference. There is no test member anywhere in the estate, an absence established by the census in [the current-state architecture](../current-state/02-architecture-current-state.md#what-is-absent), so the conversion has no behavioural net beneath it and leans on whatever the toolchain supplies. The claim that Python changes cannot be verified mechanically would be wrong: type annotations, standalone static type checkers and editor-driven rename and extract refactorings all exist and are widely used. What is true is weaker and still material — annotations are optional and are not enforced at run time, coverage of them is a property of the codebase rather than of the language, and a refactoring tool's guarantees degrade wherever they are absent or wherever behaviour is reached dynamically. That is why `C4` scores three rather than five, one below the statically typed candidates, and on a project arriving with a comprehensive suite this criterion would carry far less against it.
- **COBOL conversion tooling depth is thin.** No catalogue consulted lists an established COBOL-to-Python translator comparable to the Java and C# offerings, so `C3` scores one.

Where it is genuinely strong is worth recording rather than omitting, because it is a real role in this programme: data reconciliation and migration-validation scripting during the parallel run — extract comparison, checksum and count reconciliation, exception triage — is work Python suits well, and nothing in this recommendation excludes it from that. Choosing a language for the transactional core is not the same as choosing every language used in the programme.

**Verdict.** Evaluated and rejected for the core. A legitimate supporting role in migration validation and reconciliation.

### Go

Go's strengths are real and pull in a direction this estate cares about. It is statically typed with fast, dependable tooling, and its concurrency and operational simplicity make it a strong choice for long-running services — which is why it scores four on batch and long-running-job maturity, ahead of two of the other candidates, level with a third, and behind only the recommended one. Its release policy is published and its security and dependency tooling is sound. Its four on desirability is a declared judgement on the same footing as every other score on that criterion; no candidate-level desirability figure is reproduced from any survey in this document.

It does not pass the filter for the most basic of reasons: the language has no decimal type, and neither does its standard library. Exact decimal arithmetic requires a third-party package, and the standard database interface has no decimal type to scan a fixed-scale column into, so each such column has to be wired to the package's own scanner explicitly. For a schema whose monetary columns are pervasive and whose scales are not uniform [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L49], that is a per-column obligation rather than a one-time configuration, which is why `C8` scores two. On the two decisive labour criteria it is the weakest of the five: its pool is concentrated in infrastructure and platform engineering rather than insurance back-office systems, and no COBOL-to-Go conversion tooling appears in any catalogue consulted.

**Verdict.** Evaluated and not selected. Well suited to operational and infrastructure services; not suited to a policy-administration core whose acceptance condition is exact monetary parity.

## Primary recommendation and runner-up

**The primary recommendation is Java on a current long-term-support release, with Spring Boot**, on PostgreSQL as the relational datastore. The runner-up is C#/.NET, recorded as a candidate rather than as a second named stack. TypeScript/Node and Python are evaluated and rejected for the core. Go is evaluated and not selected.

**The recommendation names a language and a support line, not a release number.** That granularity is what the analysis supports rather than a hedge: every criterion that decides the outcome — the decimal type, the labour pool, the conversion tooling, the batch maturity — is a property of the language and its ecosystem rather than of any one feature release, so no score above would move if one release were named instead of another, and naming one would record a choice this evidence does not make. What the analysis does establish about releases is a **rule** rather than a number: the target runs on a release its publisher designates long-term-support and that is inside the support window that publisher states for it, which is a condition a reader can test at the moment of reading. That rule, the publisher of each support statement, and what it leaves for an adopting implementation to select and record are carried by [MOD-ADR-001](../decisions/MOD-ADR-001-target-language-and-runtime.md#what-this-record-fixes-and-what-it-leaves-open). The datastore is decided the same way by the planned [MOD-ADR-003](../decisions/MOD-ADR-003-target-datastore.md), not here and not by default.

The three criteria carrying the heaviest weights are **exact decimal arithmetic**, **enterprise labour-pool depth for regulated back-office work** and **COBOL-conversion tooling maturity**, and they are decisive in two different senses that are worth separating rather than blurring. Two of them — the first and the third — are what separate the leading candidate from the runner-up, and they account for thirty-five of the thirty-six points between them. The second separates the two leading candidates from the other three but **does not separate them from each other**, because the evidence behind it is an adoption proxy that names one of them and is silent rather than negative about the other. Each is decisive for a different reason:

- Exact decimal arithmetic is decisive because it is a correctness gate rather than a preference. Money in this estate is fifteen digits with two decimals, declared independently on both sides of the same field [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L52], [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L98-L105], computed by truncating expressions with no rounding phrase anywhere in the source, and read back to policyholders through a single signed field [QCPYSRC/POLDATA.cpy:L106].
- Enterprise labour-pool depth for regulated back-office work is decisive because it is the driver, restated as a property something can be said about. It is answered by attributed third-party evidence rather than by assertion, which is what `SC-2.2` requires of it — OpenLogic's finding that the banking, insurance and financial-services sector reports the highest Java usage of any industry it surveyed, and Azul's 2026 survey describing an ecosystem in active enterprise development. **Both are adoption evidence and neither measures hireable supply**, which is why no candidate scores five on this criterion, why the two leading candidates tie on it, and why confirming the position in the organisation's own recruiting markets is named here as work for the business rather than presented as settled.
- COBOL-conversion tooling maturity is decisive because the estate has no test member to guard a conversion, as the census in [the current-state architecture](../current-state/02-architecture-current-state.md#what-is-absent) establishes, so every hand-converted line is unguarded. The tooling that names this platform family targets Java most broadly and C# more narrowly, and nothing consulted targets the other three at all. It is decisive in a bounded sense and the bound is stated rather than glossed: of the five components this criterion is defined over, published offerings address the procedural logic, the database definition and — in one case — the CL layer, while the presentation contract is addressed only as a like-for-like rendering and the operational reconstruction and the characterization suite are addressed by nothing. **Tooling reduces the hand conversion; it does not remove the need for the behavioural net**, which is why that net is a precondition rather than a parallel activity.

**The datastore half of the recommendation.** PostgreSQL is named for two reasons, one internal and one external. The internal reason is that the target needs a fixed-scale exact numeric column type that the language's decimal type maps to without loss, which is the `C8` criterion, and the transformation of each legacy column into such a type is owned by [the target schema mapping](../target-state/03-target-data-model-and-schema-mapping.md) rather than by this document. Persistence in the legacy system is defined entirely by DDS: three keyed physical files — the policy master [QDDSSRC/POLMST.pf:L81], the servicing file [QDDSSRC/SVCPF.pf:L54] and the claims file [QDDSSRC/CLMPF.pf:L67] — reached by record-level I/O, with no query language, no SQL definition and no schema artifact other than the DDS members themselves. The target datastore is therefore a new choice rather than a migration of an existing one. The external reason is ecosystem depth on the same footing as the language choice, and it is adoption evidence with the same limitation: the Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025, published 29 July 2025, reports PostgreSQL as the highest-ranked database of that edition, wanted by 47 per cent of respondents for the following year and used-and-retained by 66 per cent, and states it has led that ranking for three consecutive years. That half of the recommendation feeds the planned [MOD-ADR-003](../decisions/MOD-ADR-003-target-datastore.md); the language and runtime half feeds the written [MOD-ADR-001](../decisions/MOD-ADR-001-target-language-and-runtime.md).

**Spring Boot** is named as the server-side application framework because it is where the batch and relational-access maturity scored under `C7` and `C8` actually lives, and because OpenLogic's report places its adoption highest in the banking, insurance and financial-services sector at 47.22 per cent. Amazon Web Services describing its own COBOL transformation output as Spring Boot applications is a secondary reason and a weaker one than it first appears: that service targets mainframe workloads rather than IBM i, so it evidences that the recommended stack is a conventional destination for COBOL conversion generally and evidences nothing about this platform. It is a server-side runtime choice, not a user-interface choice, and it implies nothing about component selection.

**This recommendation is supersedable.** It is recorded as a decision record with a status, not asserted as a fact, and a later record may supersede it by naming a different language on better evidence. Two criteria are most likely to move, and both would move on evidence this assessment could not obtain rather than on argument. `C2` would move on a hiring measurement — a job-posting census or a vacancy-fill figure for back-office insurance work by language — which would replace the adoption proxy both leading candidates are currently scored on and could separate them in either direction. `C3` would move on a published result from an actual ILE COBOL conversion covering the CL, DDS, presentation and operational components together, which nothing consulted supplies for any candidate.

## What this decision does not settle

Naming a language settles less than it appears to, and four adjacent decisions are deliberately left open so that no reader mistakes one for the other.

- **Where the target runs.** Hosting is not decided here, and neither is the exit from the current hardware. Both are to be deferred in the planned [MOD-ADR-007](../decisions/MOD-ADR-007-hardware-exit-deferred.md), and [the platform and support status document](../current-state/03-platform-and-support-status.md) explains why coupling a language decision to a hardware decision inflates scope and stalls work that could proceed on its own evidence. A language choice and a hardware choice rest on different evidence and have different blast radii.
- **What the user interface is built from.** No component library and no design system is named anywhere in this assessment. The estate's only interface is a fixed-format character interface on the [5250 datastream](../reference/glossary-ibm-i.md#5250-datastream), and mapping it to target screens — along with the component selection this document does not make — is owned by [the UI modernization document](../target-state/05-ui-modernization.md), which records the absence as an open gap rather than resolving it by implication.
- **How each column is typed.** This document establishes that the target requires a configurable-scale exact decimal type and a real date type. Which target type each of the legacy columns becomes, and what transformation each requires, is the column-by-column work owned by [the target schema mapping](../target-state/03-target-data-model-and-schema-mapping.md) and the representation decision to be recorded in the planned [MOD-ADR-006](../decisions/MOD-ADR-006-date-and-decimal-representation.md).
- **Which migration strategy carries the conversion.** Choosing a target language is not choosing a route to it. The strategy archetypes, their evaluation against this estate and the selection among them are owned by [the strategy options and selection document](../migration/01-strategy-options-and-selection.md).

Two further boundaries are worth stating because crossing them would exceed what the requirement authorises. **No supplier is selected and no tool is procured** — conversion-tooling maturity is weighed here as a property of an ecosystem, and the vendors named above are named only as the publishers of figures this document attributes to them. **No regulatory framework is asserted to apply** to this system; whether any does is a question the business must confirm, and it is not answered here in either direction.

Where a source concerns a platform other than this one, that limit is disclosed too, because the difference between evidence about COBOL and evidence about *this* COBOL is the difference between a general argument and one about LIFE400.

**Primary sources — the publisher's own pages.**

- Stack Overflow — *2025 Developer Survey*, `survey.stackoverflow.co/2025` and `survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/technology`, announced at `stackoverflow.co/company/press/archive/stack-overflow-2025-developer-survey` on 29 July 2025. Fifteenth edition; more than 49,000 responses from 177 countries across 62 questions and 314 technologies.
